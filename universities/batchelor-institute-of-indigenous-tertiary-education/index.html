Batchelor Institute: Indigenous Knowledge, Linguistics & Community Health Guide

Established in 1974 in the Northern Territory, Batchelor Institute of Indigenous Tertiary Education is Australia's only First Nations specialist dual-sector higher education institution. Governed by an Indigenous council, Batchelor delivers world-class postgraduate research degrees, language documentation through the Centre for Australian Languages and Linguistics (CALL), and culturally grounded education in environmental management, public health, and creative arts.

Verified degree programs and opportunities

Review checked academic pathways and funding options below:

Batchelor Institute Indigenous Research Training Program (RTP) Scholarship

Prestigious doctoral scholarship supporting domestic and international research candidates conducting community-led research in First Nations epistemologies, linguistics, and land care.

Master of Indigenous Knowledge and Community Development

Two-year postgraduate degree employing the 'Both-ways' philosophy to combine traditional Indigenous knowledge systems with Western academic analytical frameworks.

Doctor of Philosophy (PhD) in Australian Indigenous Languages and Archiving

Four-year doctoral appointment at the Centre for Australian Languages and Linguistics (CALL) conducting phonological analysis, language revitalization, and digital audio archiving.

Kakadu & Arnhem Land Indigenous Fire Management & Carbon Abatement Project

Collaborative environmental research with Traditional Owners evaluating traditional early dry-season fire regimes to reduce late dry-season wildfire emissions and earn carbon credits.

Top End Aboriginal Community Health and Epidemiology Practicum

Sixteen-week clinical practicum in remote Top End community clinics delivering culturally secure primary healthcare and evaluating chronic kidney disease prevention programs.

Batchelor Postdoctoral Fellowship in First Nations Oral History Digitization

Two-year postdoctoral research appointment curating digital archival repositories and developing community protocols for the ethical preservation of sacred oral histories.

Northern Territory Research Excellence International Scholarship

Institutional scholarship granting tuition deductions to international students enrolling in creative arts, language studies, and community education degrees at Batchelor Institute.

Admissions, language requirements & application process

Master's admission eligibility
Accredited Bachelor's degree (AQF Level 7) in humanities, health, education, or environmental management with credit average (65%+) or equivalent professional experience.
Doctoral admission eligibility
First Class Honours (AQF Level 8) or Master's degree by research with substantial original thesis; demonstration of culturally responsive research capability.
Language proficiency
IELTS Academic 6.5 overall (minimum 6.0 in each sub-test), PTE Academic 58+, or verified completion of Australian tertiary education.
Research statement / Proposal
Comprehensive 2,000-word doctoral proposal demonstrating consultation with relevant Indigenous communities and alignment with Batchelor research themes.
Application fee
$0 AUD for online direct postgraduate applications.
Required documentation
Certified academic transcripts, degree awards, research curriculum vitae, statement of community engagement, and two academic referee reports.

Tuition fees and verified scholarship systems

Australian domestic research students receive Australian Government Research Training Program (RTP) fee offsets. International tuition for postgraduate degrees is approximately $28,000 AUD to $32,000 AUD per year. Fully funded Australian Commonwealth RTP scholarships and Batchelor Research Bursaries provide $32,500 AUD/year tax-free stipends and full tuition fee remissions.
