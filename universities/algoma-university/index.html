Algoma University: Degrees, Scholarships & Faculty Guide

Algoma University, located in Sault Ste. Marie, Ontario at the intersection of Lakes Superior and Huron, is a public university recognized for its special mission in cross-cultural learning, Indigenous education in partnership with the Shingwauk Education Trust, computer game development at the Great Lakes Forestry Centre interface, and environmental freshwater ecology.

Verified degree programs and opportunities

Review checked academic pathways and funding options below:

Master of Science (M.Sc.) in Computer Game Technology and Interactive Media

Two-year specialized graduate curriculum combining 3D real-time rendering, procedural generation, game physics engines, and artificial intelligence for non-player character behaviour.

Collaborative Doctoral Fellowship in Great Lakes Aquatic Ecology & Boreal Watershed Science

Four-year doctoral candidacy hosted in collaboration with regional federal research centres, analyzing invasive fish species management, benthic macroinvertebrates, and microplastics in Lake Superior.

Shingwauk Anishinaabe Cross-Cultural Leadership Graduate Scholarship

Merit-based award celebrating cross-cultural community engagement, Indigenous rights legal frameworks, and sustainable community empowerment across Northern Ontario.

Invasive Species Research Institute (ISRI) Forest Entomology Research Fellowship

Applied research residency at the Invasive Species Research Institute developing biocontrol mechanisms, pheromone-based mating disruption, and molecular assays for emerald ash borer surveillance.

Sault Ste. Marie Innovation Centre (SSMIC) Geospatial Data Science Practicum

Four-to-six month work-integrated internship delivering spatial analytics, municipal infrastructure modeling, and satellite vegetation monitoring for Northern Ontario public utilities.

Master of Science (M.Sc.) in Biology (Northern Freshwater Ecology & Conservation)

Thesis-based two-year graduate degree exploring fish population genomics, toxic cyanobacterial bloom mitigation, and boreal wetland bio-monitoring along the Great Lakes basin.

Shingwauk Residential Schools Centre (SRSC) Postdoctoral Fellowship in Archival Truth and Healing

Two-year postdoctoral fellowship at the SRSC leading participatory oral history digitization, community survivor reconciliation access, and public educational curriculum development.

Admissions, language requirements & application process

Master's admission eligibility
A four-year bachelor's degree in a relevant discipline with a minimum cumulative average of 75% (B+ equivalent) over the final two years of study.
Doctoral admission eligibility
Master's degree or equivalent with minimum 80% standing; accepted research thesis statement and faculty supervisor pre-endorsement.
Language proficiency
IELTS Academic 6.5 overall (minimum 6.0 in each band) or TOEFL iBT 83 (minimum 20 in writing).
Research statement / Proposal
A 3-5 page research outline detailing the problem statement, methodological design, environmental/Northern focus, and lab facility needs.
Application fee
CAD $125 non-refundable application fee.
Required documentation
Official academic transcripts, graduation certificates, CV, 3 confidential letters of recommendation, and proof of English proficiency.

Tuition fees and verified scholarship systems

International graduate tuition at Algoma University averages CAD $19,000 to $23,000 per year. Admitted research graduate students qualify for Algoma University Graduate Research Assistantships (GRAs), Ontario Graduate Scholarships (OGS, CAD $15,000), and Northern Ontario Heritage Fund Corporation (NOHFC) research stipends averaging CAD $20,000 to $24,000 per year.
