Universiteit Leiden (Leiden University)

Founded in 1575 by William, Prince of Orange, Universiteit Leiden is the oldest university in the Netherlands and a foundational member of the League of European Research Universities (LERU). Renowned for educating 16 Nobel laureates (including Hendrik Lorentz, Pieter Zeeman, Heike Kamerlingh Onnes, and Jan Tinbergen), Leiden is a world leader in Astronomy (at the historic Leiden Observatory), Theoretical Physics (Lorentz Institute), Bio-pharmaceutical Sciences, and International Law in The Hague. Verified degree programs and opportunities

Review checked academic pathways and funding options below:

Leiden University Excellence Scholarships (LExS)

Prestigious university scholarship awarded to outstanding non-EU/EEA international students admitted to Master's degree programs at Leiden University, providing full tuition coverage and living allowance grants.

Leiden Salaried PhD Positions (Promovendi under CAO-NU)

Four-year salaried doctoral research appointments across Leiden University institutes, conducting frontier scientific investigations under standard Dutch collective employment contracts.

Leiden Master of Science in Computer Science

Two-year Master of Science degree (120 ECTS) at the Leiden Institute of Advanced Computer Science (LIACS), offering specializations in Artificial Intelligence, Data Science, and Computer Science & Advanced Data Analytics.

Leiden Master of Science in Astronomy

World-renowned two-year Master's degree (120 ECTS) based at the historic Leiden Observatory (founded in 1633), offering tracks in Astronomy & Astrophysics, Cosmology, Instrumentation, and Data Science.

Lorentz Institute for Theoretical Physics Research Placements

Frontier theoretical physics institute in Leiden (founded in 1921), developing foundational theories of topological superconductors, Majorana fermions, quantum computation, and quantum transport.

Leiden Bio Science Park Academic & Industrial Internships

Industrial and academic graduation research internships embedding graduate students within the Netherlands' largest life sciences innovation cluster, partnering with Janssen, Galapagos, and university research institutes.

Leiden Student Assistantships (Student-Assistenten)

Part-time campus employment for enrolled students coaching exercise sessions, running computer laboratory sessions, and assisting faculty in grading assignments.

Admissions, language requirements & application process

Master's admission eligibility
Accredited Bachelor of Science degree in Computer Science, Astronomy, Physics, or related quantitative field (minimum 180 ECTS credits) matching specific foundational course requirements with minimum GPA equivalent to Dutch 7.5/10.
Doctoral admission eligibility
Recognized Master of Science degree (120 ECTS) with excellent academic marks, strong thesis research evidence, and successful competitive interview for an advertised doctoral vacancy.
Language proficiency
Demonstrated English proficiency: Academic IELTS 7.0 overall (minimum 6.5 in all subscores) or TOEFL iBT 100 overall (minimum 22 in each component).
Research statement / Proposal
For Master's: motivation letter, curriculum vitae, and detailed course descriptions via uSis. For PhD: comprehensive research statement aligned with advertised vacancy calls.
Application fee
€100 application fee for international applicants holding non-Dutch degrees (refunded upon successful matriculation).
Required documentation
Complete official transcripts, degree certificate, detailed descriptions of core undergraduate mathematics/programming modules, curriculum vitae, and passport scan.

Tuition fees and verified scholarship systems

Under Dutch national higher education regulations, EU/EEA students pay the statutory tuition fee of €2,530 per year. For non-EU/EEA international students, tuition is approximately €19,600 to €21,500 per year for Master's programs. Outstanding admitted international students can receive the Leiden University Excellence Scholarship (LExS), covering up to 100% of tuition fees plus living allowances. Doctoral candidates (Promovendi) are paid university employees under the Dutch CAO-NU collective agreement.
