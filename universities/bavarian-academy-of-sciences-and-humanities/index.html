Bavarian Academy of Sciences and Humanities: Quantum Computing & Geodesy Guide

Founded in 1759 by Elector Max III Joseph in Munich, the Bavarian Academy of Sciences and Humanities (Bayerische Akademie der Wissenschaften - BAdW) is Germany's largest and one of Europe's most historic scientific academies. Housing the Leibniz Supercomputing Centre (LRZ), the Walther Meissner Institute for Low Temperature Research (WMI), and the Bavarian Research Institute for Digital Transformation (bidt), BAdW conducts world-class research in quantum computing, geodesy, and humanities.

Verified degree programs and opportunities

Review checked academic pathways and funding options below:

Leibniz Supercomputing Centre (LRZ) Quantum Computing Doctoral Fellowship

Doctoral research position at LRZ Garching working on the Munich Quantum Valley initiative, integrating superconducting quantum processors into hybrid exascale supercomputing pipelines.

Master of Science in Quantum Science and Technology (TUM-LMU-BAdW)

Elite two-year joint master's program delivered by TUM and LMU Munich in partnership with BAdW covering quantum information, quantum cryptography, and solid-state quantum devices.

Walther Meissner Institute Salaried PhD in Superconducting Quantum Circuits

Four-year salaried doctoral research appointment at the Walther Meissner Institute fabricating circuit quantum electrodynamics (cQED) chips and characterizing decoherence mechanisms.

BAdW Commission for Geodesy and Glaciology Alpine Ice Mass Project

High-precision geophysical research investigation utilizing ground-penetrating radar and satellite altimetry to quantify volume loss across Bavarian Alpine glaciers (Vernagtferner).

Leibniz Supercomputing Centre (LRZ) High Performance Computing Internship

Six-month research internship at the SuperMUC-NG petascale supercomputer facility optimizing parallel astrophysics and climate simulation codes for next-generation GPU accelerators.

BAdW Postdoctoral Fellowship in Quantum Nanophotonics and Diamond Defects

Two-year postdoctoral research appointment developing optically active nitrogen-vacancy (NV) and silicon-vacancy colour centres in diamond for nanoscale quantum sensing and magnetic resonance.

Bavarian Research Institute for Digital Transformation (bidt) Fellowship

Interdisciplinary research fellowship at bidt Munich investigating societal, economic, and legal transformations triggered by generative artificial intelligence and autonomous technologies.

Admissions, language requirements & application process

Master's admission eligibility
Accredited Bachelor of Science in physics, computer science, mathematics, or geosciences (minimum 180 ECTS credits) from a recognized university.
Doctoral admission eligibility
Completed Master of Science (300 ECTS total academic qualification) with distinction; admission awarded in conjunction with affiliated partner universities (TUM or LMU Munich).
Language proficiency
German proficiency at DSH-2 or TestDaF 4 for humanities projects; full English proficiency (CEFR C1 / IELTS 7.0+) for quantum physics and LRZ supercomputing research.
Research statement / Proposal
Doctoral research synopsis (10-15 pages) outlining quantum hardware architectures, high-performance computing algorithms, or geodetic satellite geodesy.
Application fee
EUR 0 for scientific academy appointments.
Required documentation
Certified master's degree diploma, complete transcripts of records, CV, publication list, three letters of recommendation, and research proposal.

Tuition fees and verified scholarship systems

Doctoral and postdoctoral researchers at BAdW and LRZ are fully salaried under German public service collective wage agreements (TV-L E13 to TV-L E14). Annual gross salaries range from approximately EUR 52,000 to EUR 65,000 with complete state health and pension benefits. Standard student semester fees at partner universities (LMU/TUM) are approximately EUR 85 to EUR 150.
