Australian Maritime College: Marine Engineering, Autonomous Vessels & Naval Architecture Guide

The Australian Maritime College (AMC) at the University of Tasmania is Australia's national institute for maritime education, research, and training, globally recognized for world-class hydrodynamic testing facilities, autonomous underwater robotics, and maritime logistics.

Verified degree programs and opportunities

Review checked academic pathways and funding options below:

Australian Government Research Training Program (RTP) Maritime Fellowship

Prestigious national postgraduate award funding Master by Research and PhD candidates investigating marine hydrodynamics, naval architecture, and coastal engineering.

Master of Maritime Engineering (Naval Architecture & Marine Engineering)

Engineers Australia-accredited Master of Maritime Engineering offering specialized streams in Naval Engineering, Ocean Engineering, and Coastal Environmental Technology.

Ph.D. in Autonomous Underwater Vehicles & Subsea Robotics

Doctoral research position utilizing AMC's deep-water towing tank and autonomous marine test bed to develop navigation and SLAM algorithms for AUVs in extreme ocean environments.

Experimental Hydrodynamics & Cavitation Tunnel Wave Energy Project

Experimental research study conducting scale model tests of offshore renewable wave energy converters inside the AMC Cavitation Tunnel and Model Test Basin.

Offshore Renewable Energy & Maritime Operations Industry Practicum

Structured twelve-week engineering internship embedded with major offshore wind developers, naval shipyards, and port authorities across Australasia.

AMC Maritime Decarbonization & Clean Hydrogen Marine Propulsion Fellowship

Three-year postdoctoral research fellowship focusing on ammonia/hydrogen fuel cell marine powertrains and hull friction reduction technologies.

Lecturer / Senior Lecturer in Naval Architecture & Marine Simulation

Tenured academic role leading curriculum development and research operations in full-mission bridge simulation, vessel hydrostatics, and computational fluid mechanics.

Admissions, language requirements & application process

Master's admission eligibility
Recognized Bachelor degree in Engineering, Naval Architecture, Marine Science, or Ocean Technology with minimum 65% (Credit) average.
Doctoral admission eligibility
Honours Class I or IIA degree or Master's by Research with proven experimental or computational marine hydrodynamic publication track record.
Language proficiency
IELTS Academic overall band 6.5 (no individual band score below 6.0) or PTE Academic 58.
Research statement / Proposal
Detailed 1,500-word doctoral research project proposal aligning with AMC hydrodynamic flume, cavitation tunnel, or maritime autonomy labs.
Application fee
Zero application fee for direct international and domestic online applications.
Required documentation
Certified academic transcripts, degree certificates, detailed curriculum vitae, and two academic referee reports.

Tuition fees and verified scholarship systems

Domestic undergraduate and postgraduate coursework places are Commonwealth Supported (CSP) or AUD $9,000 annually. International student fees range from AUD $34,000 to $42,000 per year. Fully funded Australian Government Research Training Program (RTP) scholarships provide AUD $33,500 tax-free per year.
