Marie Cederschiöld University: Palliative Care, Social Work & Salaried PhD

Marie Cederschiöld University (Marie Cederschiöld högskola - formerly Ersta Sköndal Bräcke University College), founded in 1851 in Stockholm, is an independent, non-profit Swedish higher-education institution with accredited doctoral granting authority. Famous for founding Sweden's first nursing and deaconess school, it is a national leader in palliative care, social work, ethics, and civil society research.

Salaried Swedish Doctoral Position (PhD) in Palliative Care and Healthcare Sciences

Four-year salaried doctoral research appointment within the Palliative Research Centre (PRC), evaluating symptom management, family bereavement, and ethical communication.

Swedish Institute (SI) Scholarship for Global Professionals at Marie Cederschiöld

Prestigious Swedish Government scholarship fully funding international professionals pursuing master's degrees in social welfare, civil society leadership, or health systems.

Master of Science in Caring Sciences with Specialization in Palliative Care

Advanced graduate program exploring specialized symptom management, ethical decision-making, patient-centered palliative frameworks, and holistic psychosocial support.

Palliative Research Centre (PRC) Clinical Research Assistantship

Clinical research assistantship analyzing longitudinal quality-of-life data and patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs) among advanced cancer and chronic illness cohorts.

Stockholm Hospice & Clinical Palliative Care Specialized Practicum

Ten-week advanced clinical placement embedded in specialized palliative inpatient hospices and advanced home healthcare units (ASIH) across Stockholm County.

Postdoctoral Fellowship in Civil Society Studies & Non-Profit Governance

Two-year postdoctoral position within the Institute for Civil Society Studies, analyzing non-profit contracting, volunteer participation trends, and Scandinavian welfare mix models.

Master of Science in Social Work: Leadership in Human Service Organizations

Graduate degree focusing on organization theory, ethical leadership, evidence-based social interventions, and quality management in human service delivery.

Admissions, language requirements & application process

Master's admission eligibility
Recognized Bachelor's degree (Swedish Kandidatexamen, 180 ECTS credits) in nursing, social work, public health, or related social/health science.
Doctoral admission eligibility
Master's degree (minimum 240 ECTS credits including an independent scientific thesis) with competitive selection based on research excellence and clinical insight.
Language proficiency
English 6 / English B (IELTS 6.5 with no band below 5.5, or TOEFL iBT 90 with minimum 20 in writing); Swedish proficiency required for clinical nursing practice.
Research statement / Proposal
Doctoral applications require a 3,000-word research proposal focused on palliative care, healthcare sciences, or social civil society studies.
Application fee
SEK 900 application fee paid via the national UniversityAdmissions.se portal (EU/EEA students exempt).
Required documentation
Bachelor's and master's certificates, official transcripts of records, proof of English language proficiency, CV, and statement of research purpose.

Tuition fees and verified scholarship systems

Tuition is 100% free for citizens of Sweden, the EU, EEA, and Switzerland. For non-EU/EEA international students, master's tuition is approximately SEK 120,000 per academic year. All doctoral positions are salaried Swedish university contracts (starting at approximately SEK 33,000 per month with zero tuition and full social security benefits).
