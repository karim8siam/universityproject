University of Birmingham

Founded in 1900 by Royal Charter as Britain's first civic or 'redbrick' university, the University of Birmingham is a founding member of the Russell Group and Universitas 21. Birmingham has been the birthplace of major world-changing breakthroughs including the cavity magnetron (radar), pacemaker technologies, and gravitational wave instrumentation. The university is home to the national UK Quantum Technology Hub Sensors and Timing, the Birmingham Centre for Railway Research and Education, and the Institute of Microbiology and Infection.

Birmingham UK Quantum Technology Hub Doctoral Training Studentships

Doctoral training at the £80 million UK Quantum Technology Hub Sensors and Timing, developing ultra-precise cold-atom gravimeters, quantum clocks, and quantum gravity sensors for subterranean surveying.

Birmingham Master of Science (MSc) in Advanced Computer Science

Advanced one-year MSc accredited by the British Computer Society (BCS), offering specialized research modules in deep learning, natural language processing, cryptography, and complex systems.

Birmingham PhD in Immunology and Immunotherapy

Doctoral research within the Institute of Immunology and Immunotherapy, developing chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T-cell therapies, cancer vaccines, and autoimmune treatments.

Birmingham Centre for Railway Research and Education (BCRRE) PhD in Railway Systems

Europe's largest university-based railway research center, investigating hydrogen-powered rolling stock, automated train operation (ATO), and resilient high-speed rail infrastructure.

Birmingham Institute for Global Innovation Postdoctoral Fellowship

Two-year postdoctoral fellowship addressing UN Sustainable Development Goals, clean urban air governance, and equitable global health policy in emerging economies.

Birmingham School of Chemical Engineering Summer Research Fellowship

Eight-week mentored summer research project investigating microencapsulation, sustainable food formulations, and carbon-neutral chemical processes.

Birmingham Lecturer (Assistant Professor) in Clean Energy Technologies & Fuel Cells

Tenured-track Assistant Professorship (Lectureship) delivering teaching in chemical engineering and leading independent laboratory research in fuel cell technologies, hydrogen generation, and electrochemical energy storage.

Admissions, language requirements & application process

Master's admission eligibility
UK Upper Second Class Honours degree (2:1) or international equivalent (minimum 65-70% or 3.2/4.0 GPA) in a relevant discipline; quantitative computing programs require strong mathematical background.
Doctoral admission eligibility
Recognized Master's degree with Merit or Distinction (or UK 2:1 Honours degree with significant research experience) and demonstrated academic research aptitude.
Language proficiency
Academic IELTS 6.5 overall (minimum 6.0 in each component) or TOEFL iBT 88; Computer Science, Law, and Medicine require IELTS 7.0 (minimum 6.5 in all bands).
Research statement / Proposal
A formal research proposal of 1,500 to 2,500 words detailing research objectives, context, methodology, proposed timeline, and prospective Birmingham academic supervisor.
Application fee
£50 application fee for taught postgraduate programs; £0 application fee for postgraduate research (PhD) applications.
Required documentation
Complete official academic transcripts, degree certificates, 2 academic reference letters, detailed research proposal, and personal statement.

Tuition fees and verified scholarship systems

Full-time doctoral researchers awarded UKRI (EPSRC, BBSRC, MRC, ESRC) studentships or Birmingham Doctoral Scholarships receive 100% full tuition fee coverage (at home/international rates) and an annual tax-free living stipend of at least £19,237 per year (aligned with UKRI national minimum rate) plus an annual research training support grant (RTSG) for laboratory consumables and conference travel.
