Institutional Overview & Campus Profile

Located in the iconic stained-glass building at the Media Park in Hilversum with an additional museum site in The Hague, the Netherlands Institute for Sound and Vision (Nederlands Instituut voor Beeld en Geluid) is Europe's largest audiovisual media archive. It functions as an accredited national research institute and university consortium partner, steward to over one million hours of television, radio, music, and digital games, and a pioneer in AI-assisted media archiving, news literacy, and digital cultural analytics.

Institutional Research Strengths

  • Artificial intelligence for audiovisual data mining, automated speech-to-text, and computer vision
  • Digital humanities and big data analysis of Dutch broadcasting history
  • Media literacy, digital disinformation tracking, and journalistic ethics
  • Video game preservation, interactive media archiving, and virtual reality heritage

Featured Academic Degrees & Professional Programs

  • Sound & Vision Visiting Research Fellowship in Digital Audiovisual Heritage (Postdoctoral Fellowship) — Three-to-six-month residential fellowship utilizing computational access to the 1-million-hour Dutch national media archive.
  • CLARIAH Graduate Research Internship in Media Analytics & NLP (Graduate Practicum) — Collaborative master's research placement applying natural language processing and computer vision to historical news broadcasts.
  • Executive Masterclass in AI-Driven Audiovisual Archiving & FAIR Data (Professional Certificate) — Specialized training for broadcast archivists, librarians, and museum curators on automated metadata extraction.
