Nungalinya College: Indigenous Leadership, First Nations Languages & Community Guide

Established in 1973 in Casuarina (Darwin), Northern Territory, Nungalinya College is a combined-church higher educational institution and Registered Training Organisation owned by the Anglican, Uniting, and Catholic churches. Dedicated to empowering Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Australians, Nungalinya provides culturally safe, nationally accredited tertiary education in Indigenous leadership, community management, First Nations languages, and theology.

Nungalinya Indigenous Leadership and Community Elders Bursary

Endowed scholarship ensuring full financial coverage, residential lodging, and travel for Indigenous leaders travelling from remote communities to study in Darwin.

Diploma of Indigenous Community Leadership and Governance

Accredited higher vocational diploma equipping emerging First Nations directors with skills in non-profit board governance, financial accountability, and dispute mediation.

CDU-Nungalinya Collaborative Doctoral Fellowship in First Nations Linguistics

Collaborative PhD candidature conducted with Charles Darwin University researching phonetic structures, morphology, and dictionary compilation for northern Australian languages.

Nungalinya Indigenous Art & Cultural Knowledge Preservation Project

Research project documenting sacred iconography, songlines, and intergenerational transmission of traditional weaving and ochre techniques across Arnhem Land.

Top End Remote Community Administration and Land Council Internship

Two-month practicum placed with Aboriginal Land Councils and community health services in the Northern Territory managing regional service logistics and minutes.

Nungalinya Senior Research Fellow in Indigenous Knowledge Systems

Two-year academic research appointment investigating decolonial pedagogy, bi-cultural curriculum design, and adult literacy frameworks for remote Australian learners.

Nungalinya First Nations Women in Governance Grant

Targeted educational grant fostering female Indigenous empowerment, corporate directorship capability, and local governance leadership across remote Australia.

Admissions, language requirements & application process

Master's admission eligibility
Accredited Australian certificate, diploma, or bachelor's degree in community development, education, or theology, plus demonstrated community leadership standing in an Indigenous community.
Doctoral admission eligibility
Collaborative doctoral pathways hosted in partnership with Charles Darwin University and the Northern Institute in Indigenous knowledge systems and language preservation.
Language proficiency
Demonstrated competence in English or First Nations regional creole; English language literacy support integrated into residential study blocks.
Research statement / Proposal
Required for community research bursaries: 500-word statement articulating cultural governance objectives and proposed community project.
Application fee
$0 AUD application fee.
Required documentation
Proof of Aboriginal and/or Torres Strait Islander heritage or community endorsement, certified identification, prior educational records, and letter of church or community elder support.

Tuition fees and verified scholarship systems

Tuition is fully subsidized for qualifying Indigenous Australian students through Australian Government ABSTUDY funding, which also covers on-campus residential accommodation, return travel from remote communities, and living allowances. Institutional bursaries cover equipment and instructional materials.
