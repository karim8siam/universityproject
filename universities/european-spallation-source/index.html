European Spallation Source: Neutron Scattering & Quantum Materials Guide

Located in Lund, Sweden, the European Spallation Source (ESS) is a multi-disciplinary international research infrastructure established as a European Research Infrastructure Consortium (ERIC) by 13 member nations. Operating the world's most powerful pulsed spallation neutron source, a 5 MW linear proton accelerator, and partnering with Lund University and LINXS, ESS delivers unmatched microscopic insight into quantum materials, structural biology, and energy storage.

ESS & LINXS Doctoral Research Fellowship in Neutron Scattering on Quantum Materials

Four-year salaried doctoral research appointment based at ESS Lund and affiliated with Lund University investigating unconventional superconductivity, topological insulators, and magnetic skyrmions.

Master of Science in Synchrotron and Neutron Radiation (Lund University-ESS)

Two-year elite international master's program taught in Lund giving direct hands-on training at the MAX IV synchrotron and European Spallation Source for material characterization.

Salaried PhD Position in Linear Accelerator RF Systems & Beam Physics

Four-year salaried doctoral research employment optimizing superconducting radiofrequency (SRF) cavity cryomodules and beam halo mitigation on the 2 GeV ESS proton linear accelerator.

ESS In-Situ Solid-State Battery Neutron Diffraction & Degradation Project

Cutting-edge research collaboration at LINXS and ESS tracking real-time lithium-ion transport and dendrite formation inside commercial all-solid-state lithium batteries under rapid charging.

European Spallation Source Cryogenic Engineering & Vacuum Systems Internship

Six-month applied engineering internship at the ESS Target and Cryogenics division testing superfluid helium heat exchangers and vacuum chamber integrity for the rotating tungsten target wheel.

ESS Postdoctoral Research Fellowship in Neutron Macromolecular Crystallography

Two-year postdoctoral position utilizing neutron diffraction to determine precise hydrogen and protonation states in enzymatic catalytic pockets for antiviral drug discovery (NMX beamline).

Swedish Research Council (VR) International Young Researcher Fellowship

Prestigious Swedish government research fellowship awarded to exceptional early-career physicists to establish independent research groups at major Swedish research infrastructures.

Admissions, language requirements & application process

Master's admission eligibility
Accredited Bachelor of Science in physics, materials science, mechanical engineering, or chemistry (minimum 180 ECTS credits) via University Admissions Sweden.
Doctoral admission eligibility
Master of Science in physics or materials engineering (minimum 120 ECTS); doctoral positions are hosted jointly with Lund University, Uppsala University, or European partner universities.
Language proficiency
English 6 proficiency equivalent: IELTS Academic 6.5 overall (minimum 5.5 all bands) or TOEFL iBT 90 (minimum 20 in writing).
Research statement / Proposal
Doctoral research plan (10-15 pages) detailing neutron scattering methodology, cryogenic instrumentation, or linear accelerator beam dynamics.
Application fee
SEK 0 for ERIC research and scientific staff appointments.
Required documentation
Master's diploma, transcript of records, CV, publication list, three letters of academic reference, and statement of research purpose.

Tuition fees and verified scholarship systems

Under Swedish law and ERIC statutes, doctoral education is tuition-free. Admitted PhD researchers hold salaried employment contracts (doktorandanställning) earning approximately SEK 33,000 to SEK 38,000 per month with comprehensive Swedish social security, health, and pension benefits. Postdoctoral fellows earn between SEK 44,000 and SEK 52,000 per month.
