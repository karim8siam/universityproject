University of Bristol (Clifton, Bristol)

Founded in 1876, the University of Bristol is a premier British research institution and a founding member of the prestigious Russell Group. Ranked among the world's top 60 universities, Bristol has been the home of 13 Nobel laureates—including Paul Dirac (quantum mechanics) and Dorothy Hodgkin (crystallography). Renowned as the UK's 'silicon gorge' and a global quantum capital, Bristol leads in quantum engineering technology, composite aerospace materials, robotics, and population health science. Verified degree programs and opportunities

Review checked academic pathways and funding options below:

Bristol Quantum Engineering Centre for Doctoral Training (CDT) Studentships

Flagship 4-year cohort doctoral training center in quantum technology, training researchers in photonic quantum computing, quantum sensors, and secure quantum networks.

Bristol Master of Science (MSc) in Computer Science (Conversion)

One-year intensive conversion Master's degree teaching software development (Java, C), algorithms, computer architecture, databases, and software engineering to graduates of non-CS backgrounds.

Bristol PhD in Advanced Composites & Aerospace Structures

Four-year doctoral program at the Bristol Composites Institute (BCI) developing ultra-lightweight structural materials for net-zero commercial aviation and space launch systems.

Bristol Postdoctoral Senior Research Associate in Synthetic Biology & Biomanufacturing

Two-year postdoctoral position at BrisSynBio (one of the UK's synthetic biology research centers) engineering artificial cellular compartments and biosynthetic enzymatic cascades.

Bristol Master of Science (MSc) in Climate Change Science and Policy

One-year interdisciplinary Master's degree at the Cabot Institute for the Environment bridging geophysical climate modeling, international environmental law, and net-zero policy design.

Bristol School of Chemistry Undergraduate Summer Research Fellowship

Eight-week hands-on summer research placement in the School of Chemistry investigating catalytic reaction mechanisms and functional nanomaterials.

Bristol Lecturer / Assistant Professor in Quantitative Economics & Econometrics

Permanent tenure-track academic position at the School of Economics conducting research in applied econometrics and teaching undergraduate and postgraduate economics cohorts.

Admissions, language requirements & application process

Master's admission eligibility
UK 2:1 Honours degree (or international equivalent, typically minimum 3.3/4.0 GPA or 65%+ UK mark equivalent) in an aligned discipline.
Doctoral admission eligibility
Master's degree with Merit or Distinction (or UK 1st Class Honours undergraduate degree) with demonstrable research expertise and supervisor alignment.
Language proficiency
Academic IELTS 6.5 to 7.0 overall (minimum 6.0 to 6.5 in all bands depending on faculty profile) or TOEFL iBT 90 to 100.
Research statement / Proposal
Comprehensive 1,500 to 2,000-word research proposal articulating research questions, theoretical context, methodological framework, and prospective Bristol supervisor.
Application fee
£60 application fee for select Master's programs in the Business School and School of Economics; £0 application fee for all postgraduate research (PhD) degrees.
Required documentation
Official academic transcripts, certified English translations, degree certificates, CV, 2 academic reference letters, and research proposal.

Tuition fees and verified scholarship systems

International doctoral students are supported by University of Bristol Postgraduate Research Scholarships and EPSRC/BBSRC doctoral training partnerships. Full scholarship recipients receive a 100% full international tuition fee waiver, an annual tax-free living stipend at national UKRI rates (£21,237 per year for 3.5 to 4 years), and an annual research travel support grant.
