Queen's University Belfast: Cyber Security, Medicine & PhD Studentships

Queen's University Belfast (QUB), established in 1845 by Queen Victoria, is a prestigious member of the UK's elite Russell Group of research-intensive universities. Home to the UK's national Cyber Security Innovation Centre (CSIT), the Patrick G. Johnston Centre for Cancer Research, and the Institute for Global Food Security, Queen's produces internationally acclaimed scientific breakthroughs.

Admissions, language requirements & application process

Master's admission eligibility
First-class or upper second-class honours degree (2:1 or international equivalent / GPA 3.3+) from a recognized higher-education institution in a relevant subject.
Doctoral admission eligibility
Master's degree with merit or distinction (overall mark 60%+), plus a comprehensive 2,000-word doctoral research proposal aligned with a QUB academic supervisor.
Language proficiency
IELTS Academic 6.5 overall (minimum 5.5 in each component), TOEFL iBT 90 (minimum 18 in reading, 17 in listening, 20 in speaking, 17 in writing), or PTE Academic 62+.
Research statement / Proposal
Required for doctoral admission: 2,000-word research proposal outlining research questions, literature context, methodology, and ethical implications.
Application fee
£0 (No application fee for direct online postgraduate applications via the Queen's portal).
Required documentation
Certified degree transcripts and certificates, two academic reference letters, research proposal, CV, and valid English proficiency test certificate.

Tuition fees and verified scholarship systems

Tuition for international graduate students ranges from £20,800 to £26,500 per year for standard classroom and laboratory disciplines, and up to £40,000 for clinical programs. Queen's awards over 100 fully funded DfE and UKRI Doctoral Studentships annually covering full tuition fees and an annual tax-free living stipend of £19,237+.
