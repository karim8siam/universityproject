University of Groningen (Rijksuniversiteit Groningen)

Founded in 1614, the University of Groningen is a world top-100 classic research university in the Netherlands. Home to Nobel Prize laureates Ben Feringa and Frits Zernike, the university hosts internationally renowned research centers including the Zernike Institute for Advanced Materials, Stratingh Institute for Chemistry, and Bernoulli Institute for AI and Mathematics. Verified degree programs and opportunities

Review checked academic pathways and funding options below:

Eric Bleumink Fund (EBF) Full Scholarships

Prestigious fully funded fellowship covering 100% of Master's tuition, monthly living allowance, international airfare, and health insurance for students from eligible developing countries.

Salaried PhD Positions (CAO-NU Promovendi)

Four-year fully funded university employee research contract (€3,204–€4,051/month) plus 8% holiday allowance, 8.3% 13th-month bonus, and comprehensive state pension.

MSc in Applied Physics (Zernike Institute)

Two-year master's program (120 ECTS) affiliated with the world-leading Zernike Institute for Advanced Materials, focusing on quantum materials, nanophysics, and device physics.

MSc in Artificial Intelligence (Bernoulli Institute)

Selective two-year master's program (120 ECTS) in autonomous systems, deep learning, cognitive robotics, multi-agent modeling, and hybrid intelligence.

Stratingh & Zernike Molecular Science Research Placements

Funded research positions and master thesis projects inside laboratories pioneering synthetic molecular motors, responsive nanomaterials, and asymmetric catalysis.

CogniGron AI & Neuromorphic Research Placements

Funded internships and industrial thesis stipends (€600–€1,200/month) researching brain-inspired computing hardware and low-power machine learning models.

Student-Assistent & Graduate Teaching Assistantship

Salaried academic positions (€15.50–€18.75/hr under CAO-NU) for enrolled students assisting faculty with undergraduate science laboratory coaching and exercise teaching.

Admissions, language requirements & application process

Master's admission eligibility
Recognized academic Bachelor of Science degree in a relevant discipline with strong quantitative background and demonstrated research methodology coursework.
Doctoral (PhD) employment eligibility
Recognized second-cycle Master's degree (120 ECTS) satisfying Dutch university hiring criteria for academic researchers (promovendi).
Language proficiency
Academic IELTS 6.5 overall (with minimum 6.5 in all sub-scores for Physics; minimum 6.0 for AI) or TOEFL iBT 90.
Standardized testing (GRE/GMAT)
Not required by Dutch research universities for science and engineering master's programs.
Application fee
€100 non-refundable application fee for non-EEA applicants via the university's Online Application System (OAS).
Central registration (Studielink)
Mandatory. All degree applicants must register through the Dutch national portal Studielink before submitting OAS documents.

Tuition fees and verified scholarship systems

For European (EU/EEA) students, the Dutch statutory tuition fee is €2,694 per year. For non-EU/EEA international students, institutional tuition for Science and Engineering master's degrees is €24,900 per year. Admitted international students can be nominated for the Eric Bleumink Fund (100% full coverage) or receive the NL Scholarship (€5,000).
