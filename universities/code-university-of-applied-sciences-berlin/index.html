CODE University Berlin: Software Engineering, Product Management & Interaction Design Guide

CODE University of Applied Sciences is a state-recognized, innovative private university situated in the heart of Berlin's vibrant startup ecosystem. Established by tech entrepreneurs and educational innovators, CODE breaks away from traditional lecture-based schooling through curiosity-driven, project-based learning. Students from over 70 nations collaborate in agile product teams across three core disciplines: Software Engineering, Product Management, and Interaction Design, developing real digital products alongside industry partners including Porsche Digital, trivago, and Zalando.

Admissions, language requirements & application process

Master's admission eligibility
Currently focused on groundbreaking undergraduate bachelor's degrees and applied executive certificates; master's programs in digital transformation under development.
Doctoral admission eligibility
Not applicable; state-recognized university of applied sciences emphasizing practical digital product creation.
Language proficiency
CEFR B2/C1 English proficiency; validated through the interactive online admissions challenge and video interview (standardized tests like IELTS 6.5 accepted but not mandatory).
Research statement / Proposal
Admissions challenge: submission of a personal project, code repository, product concept, or design portfolio demonstrating curiosity, self-direction, and problem-solving.
Application fee
€0 application fee.
Required documentation
High school graduation certificate (Abitur, IB, or international university entrance qualification), curriculum vitae, admissions challenge submission, and video introduction.

Tuition fees and verified scholarship systems

Tuition is approximately €10,800 to €11,940 per academic year. CODE uniquely offers an Income Share Agreement (Chancen e.V. ISA), allowing European and eligible international students to study with €0 upfront tuition, repaying a set percentage of their income only after securing a qualifying career threshold.
