Bangor University: Degrees, Scholarships & Faculty Guide

Bangor University (Prifysgol Bangor), established in 1884 on the scenic Menai Strait in North Wales, is internationally renowned for having one of the largest and most prestigious marine science departments in Europe—the School of Ocean Sciences—along with its deep-sea research vessel 'Prince Madog'. Bangor also excels in cognitive neuroscience, forestry, and Welsh linguistics.

Verified degree programs and opportunities

Review checked academic pathways and funding options below:

Doctor of Philosophy (PhD) in Physical Oceanography and Marine Renewable Energy

Four-year doctoral candidacy in the School of Ocean Sciences modeling tidal turbine turbulence, seabed sediment transport, and ocean thermal energy conversion along the Irish Sea.

Master of Science (MSc) in Marine Biology and Coral Reef Conservation

One-year intensive master's program utilizing Bangor's coastal marine stations, wet laboratories, and research vessels to investigate marine protected areas, larval dispersal, and climate resilience.

Vice-Chancellor's International Excellence Scholarship

Bangor's most prestigious merit award for incoming international postgraduate students demonstrating academic distinction, community engagement, and clear research direction.

School of Psychology and Sport Science Cognitive Neuroscience Research Assistantship

Two-year research appointment utilizing Bangor's dedicated 3T MRI scanner and TMS laboratories, investigating social perception, empathy mechanisms, and visual attention networks.

Snowdonia National Park Upland Forest Ecology Field Research Internship

Four-month summer field practicum assessing peatland carbon re-wetting, Atlantic temperate rainforest restoration, and ancient oak woodland biodiversity across North Wales.

Nuclear Futures Institute (NFI) Early Career Fellowship in Advanced Nuclear Materials

Two-year research fellowship at the Nuclear Futures Institute researching accident-tolerant fuel clad coatings, micro-reactor designs, and thermal barrier ceramics for nuclear energy.

Master of Science (MSc) in Computing and Environmental Geospatial Data Analytics

Conversion master's program training students in software engineering, database design, environmental telemetry pipelines, and machine learning models for climate impact forecasting.

Admissions, language requirements & application process

Master's admission eligibility
A UK 2:1 or recognized international equivalent honours degree in a relevant discipline (or 2:2 with relevant professional experience).
Doctoral admission eligibility
UK Master's degree with Merit or Distinction, or first-class honours bachelor's degree; approved research proposal and supervisor commitment.
Language proficiency
IELTS Academic 6.0 to 6.5 overall (minimum 5.5 to 6.0 in all sub-bands) or equivalent Pearson PTE Academic.
Research statement / Proposal
A 1,500-2,000 word research proposal outlining scientific background, hypotheses, methodology, and marine/field equipment requirements.
Application fee
£0 application fee for direct online postgraduate research and master's submissions.
Required documentation
Degree transcripts and certificates, CV, 2 academic references, personal statement, and evidence of English language qualifications.

Tuition fees and verified scholarship systems

International postgraduate tuition at Bangor University ranges from £17,500 to £22,000 per year. Fully funded doctoral candidates through UKRI doctoral training partnerships (such as Envision and DTPs) receive complete international tuition fee waivers and tax-free annual stipends of £19,237 per year plus research training and marine fieldwork allowances.
