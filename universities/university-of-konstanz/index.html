University of Konstanz: Degree Programs, Scholarships & Research Guide

Established in 1966 on the shores of Lake Constance, the University of Konstanz (Universität Konstanz) is one of Germany's most celebrated universities of excellence. Renowned as 'Little Harvard on the Lake' for its integrated campus architecture, flat hierarchies, and exceptional research output in collective animal behavior, political science, chemistry, and condensed matter physics.

Verified degree programs and opportunities

Review checked academic pathways and funding options below:

Centre for the Advanced Study of Collective Behaviour International Fellowship

Excellence cluster scholarship funding international master's and doctoral students conducting research in animal swarm dynamics and visual tracking.

Master of Science in Biological Sciences and Molecular Ecology

English-taught master's curriculum combining molecular genetics, evolutionary toxicology, physiological plant ecology, and limnological field investigations on Lake Constance.

Max Planck Institute of Animal Behavior & Konstanz Doctoral Fellowship (TV-L E13)

Three-year doctoral research fellowship at the International Max Planck Research School (IMPRS) developing global satellite tracking algorithms (ICARUS) and behavioral computer vision.

Konstanz Collaborative Research Center (SFB 1432) Ultrafast Phenomena Project

Experimental research placement in the DFG Collaborative Research Center examining non-equilibrium quantum states and femtosecond laser-driven electronic phase transitions.

Limnological Institute Lake Constance Freshwater Ecosystems Practicum

Semester-long ecological practicum aboard the university's research vessel investigating deep alpine lake stratification, invasive quagga mussel ecology, and nutrient turnover.

Konstanz Postdoctoral Research Fellowship in Functional Polymer Colloids

Two-year postdoctoral fellowship conducting cutting-edge synthesis of recyclable polyolefins, biodegradable aliphatic polyesters, and functional catalytic nanoparticles.

W1 Junior Professorship in Quantitative Political Science and Conflict Analysis

Tenure-track assistant professorship leading an autonomous research team analyzing political violence, geospatial conflict data, and democratic institution resilience.

Admissions, language requirements & application process

Master's admission eligibility
Completed Bachelor of Science or Arts degree with at least 180 ECTS credits (or international equivalent) with an equivalent German grade of 2.5 or better.
Doctoral admission eligibility
Master of Science/Arts degree satisfying Baden-Württemberg doctoral regulations, accepted by a faculty doctoral commission with professor supervision.
Language proficiency
For English-taught master's: IELTS Academic 6.5 overall (minimum 6.0 in each subtest) or TOEFL iBT 90. For German-taught courses: DSH-2 or TestDaF 4x4.
Research statement / Proposal
Curriculum vitae, detailed bachelor module description/syllabus, and letter of motivation detailing academic research background.
Application fee
€0 (Centralized university application via the ZEuS portal).
Required documentation
Certified degree diplomas, complete transcripts with grading scales, English proficiency certificates, and copy of passport.

Tuition fees and verified scholarship systems

Under Baden-Württemberg higher education law, non-EU international students pay a statutory tuition fee of €1,500 per semester (€3,000 per academic year) plus approximately €180 semester administrative contributions. Fully employed doctoral researchers (TV-L E13) pay zero tuition and receive full public service salaries of approximately €52,000 per year.
