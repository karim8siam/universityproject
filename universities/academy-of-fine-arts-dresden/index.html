Academy of Fine Arts Dresden (HfBK)

Hochschule für Bildende Künste Dresden is a specialist public arts institution. It offers artistic degree pathways and doctoral study in defined scholarly fields; neither a doctorate route nor a scholarship listing is a funded vacancy.

Official institution source ↗Independent academic guide

Study routes: diploma, advanced artistic study and doctorate

The institution's five broad study areas are Fine Arts, Conservation/Restoration, Stage and Costume Design, Theatre Design and Art Therapy. Its artistic Meisterschüler route is an advanced course of study; it should not be treated as equivalent to a research PhD or automatically as a conventional English-language Master's degree.

The institution explicitly holds the right to award doctorates in art history and theory, philosophical aesthetics, art technology, conservation/restoration and art therapy. Doctoral study is listed for art history, philosophy/aesthetics, art therapy and conservation/restoration. A doctoral application depends on the applicable regulations and university acceptance; the existence of a subject does not prove a currently funded place.

Doctoral application: practical sequence

  1. Read the doctoral regulations and subject list for your field; academic qualification equivalence and research requirements depend on the relevant regulations.
  2. Identify a relevant scholarly department and ask whether your proposed topic and supervision fit the program. Do not assume any listed professor is accepting new students.
  3. Prepare your research proposal, previous qualifications, academic transcript and any field-specific materials required by the department.
  4. After inclusion in the doctoral list, the university describes enrollment documents including the doctoral enrollment request, application form and confirmation of admission to the doctoral list. Submit within the next semester's re-registration window as instructed by Student Affairs.
  5. Confirm language, recognition of overseas qualifications and financing directly with the university. No universal IELTS, GRE or fully funded PhD entitlement was verified on the reviewed pages.

Deutschlandstipendium: the current German-language announcement offers twelve awards across two faculties, including subject-specific awards. Funding runs 1 October 2026 to 30 September 2027. The published closing date is 14 October 2026 (today in Dresden). Applicants should verify whether the portal is still accepting submissions before relying on this deadline. The English translation currently shows an outdated 2025 date; this guide prioritizes the German announcement.

DAAD prize: the university describes a separate €1,000 prize for distinguished enrolled international students or doctoral candidates; it is not a general admission scholarship or an automatic annual payment. STIBET support is administered separately, with eligibility depending on current calls.

Project funding: the university's Friends Association publishes grants of €500 for eligible student projects, with a stated next submission date of 16 November 2026; degree-final projects are excluded from that project-grant category. This is not a PhD stipend.

Costs, language and deadlines

Semester contribution
University's 2026/27 published total: €354.91 for ordinary re-registration, including semester ticket. Special exemptions and reduced contributions apply in specified categories; always verify your own status.
Additional fees
The university lists additional €350 for Art Therapy, second-degree students, and €500 for long-term students in its published fee notice; do not generalize these charges to every doctorate or all semesters.
Fine Arts applications
The published 2026/27 Fine Arts deadline was 15 March 2026; university says a new 15 January deadline applies from 2027. Confirm the exact cycle before applying.
Meisterschüler
The 2026/27 published application deadline was 1 July 2026. A later intake deadline has not been confirmed here.
English/German requirements
Not verified as one universal language threshold for all programs. The official application rules are program-specific; check the current German requirements and ask the admissions office.

International applicant checklist

Start with the exact degree and application cycle rather than a general scholarship promise. The institution's German application forms and regulations are the authoritative documents. Before applying from abroad, confirm qualification recognition, portfolio or aptitude-test requirements, language proficiency, doctoral supervisor arrangements, semester charges and whether a scholarship is open to incoming or only already-enrolled students.

For an international student, STIBET is generally for enrolled degree students and doctoral candidates, not a guaranteed funding offer attached to admission. The Deutschlandstipendium announcement likewise specifies current enrollment conditions.
