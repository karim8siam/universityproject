Cornell University

Founded in 1865 by Ezra Cornell and Andrew Dickson White, Cornell University is an Ivy League research university and the land-grant institution of New York State. Cornell is celebrated globally for extraordinary discoveries across computing and information science (Cornell Bowers CIS), engineering, the world-leading Cornell Lab of Ornithology, physics and synchrotron science (Cornell High Energy Synchrotron Source - CHESS), and the cutting-edge Cornell Tech campus in New York City. Cornell guarantees multi-year full funding packages for all admitted doctoral students.

Cornell University Provost Diversity Fellowships & Doctoral Fellowship Packages

Cornell's flagship university-wide doctoral support guarantee, providing multi-year living stipends, comprehensive health insurance, and full tuition waivers for exceptional doctoral scholars across all disciplines.

Cornell Bowers CIS PhD in Computer Science (100% Fully Funded)

Consistently ranked among the world's top 5 computer science departments, offering doctoral training in theoretical computer science, machine learning, artificial intelligence, robotics, systems, and programming languages.

Cornell PhD in Applied Physics & Synchrotron Radiation (100% Fully Funded)

World-class doctoral research at the intersection of fundamental physics and applied technology, utilizing the Cornell High Energy Synchrotron Source (CHESS) and the Kavli Institute at Cornell for Nanoscale Science.

Cornell Master of Engineering (MEng) in Biomedical Engineering

Accelerated one-year professional engineering degree combining advanced bioengineering coursework with an intensive industry-sponsored medical device design project.

Cornell Lab of Ornithology Postdoctoral Fellowship in Biodiversity Conservation

Prestigious postdoctoral appointment at the world-renowned Cornell Lab of Ornithology, leveraging global eBird data, AI bioacoustics, and computational modeling to guide global avian and biodiversity conservation.

Cornell High Energy Synchrotron Source (CHESS) Summer Research Internship

Ten-week National Science Foundation REU summer research traineeship at CHESS, conducting hands-on synchrotron X-ray diffraction, spectroscopy, and imaging experiments on advanced materials.

Cornell Postdoctoral Associate in Plant Breeding and Genetics

Postdoctoral research appointment within Cornell's School of Integrative Plant Science (SIPS), developing genomic selection models and climate-resilient crop varieties to address global food security.

Admissions, language requirements & application process

Master's admission eligibility
Accredited Bachelor's degree with a strong academic record (typically minimum 3.5/4.0 GPA or First Class Honours); prerequisite technical coursework in relevant engineering or scientific fields.
Doctoral admission eligibility
Recognized Bachelor's or Master's degree with superior academic achievement; demonstrated capacity for independent research through publications or honors theses.
Language proficiency
Academic IELTS 7.0 overall (minimum 7.0 in speaking) or TOEFL iBT 100 (minimum 22 writing, 24 speaking, 20 reading and listening); higher for Computer Science.
Research statement / Proposal
A 2 to 3 page Statement of Purpose outlining research problems of interest, academic preparation, faculty members with whom the applicant wishes to work, and long-term career goals.
Application fee
$105 non-refundable application fee submitted via the Cornell Graduate School application system (fee waivers available for eligible applicants).
Required documentation
Complete official academic transcripts, curriculum vitae, 3 letters of academic recommendation, statement of purpose, and GRE scores (where specified by individual graduate fields).

Tuition fees and verified scholarship systems

Cornell University provides guaranteed multi-year funding packages to all admitted PhD students in good academic standing. The standard doctoral package covers 100% of full tuition ($29,500/year equivalent graduate rate), full individual student health insurance (SHIP, valued at over $3,800/year), and an annual living stipend of $44,500 to $48,000 for 9 to 12 months (combining Cornell fellowships, graduate research assistantships, and teaching assistantships).
