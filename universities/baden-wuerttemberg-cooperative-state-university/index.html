Baden-Wuerttemberg Cooperative State University: Dual Engineering & AI Guide

With headquarters in Stuttgart and campuses across Baden-Württemberg (including Karlsruhe, Mannheim, Ravensburg, and Villingen-Schwenningen), Baden-Wuerttemberg Cooperative State University (Duale Hochschule Baden-Württemberg - DHBW) is Germany's pioneer and largest dual-study higher education institution. DHBW systematically alternates 12-week academic theory phases with 12-week workplace engineering rotations at over 9,000 corporate partner enterprises including Mercedes-Benz, Porsche, SAP, and Bosch.

Admissions, language requirements & application process

Master's admission eligibility
Accredited Bachelor's degree (minimum 180 to 210 ECTS) in engineering, computer science, or business with a German grade of 2.5 or better, plus minimum one year of professional post-bachelor industry experience.
Doctoral admission eligibility
Cooperative doctoral degrees (Kooperative Promotion) conducted jointly with partner research universities (e.g. University of Stuttgart, KIT).
Language proficiency
German language proficiency at DSH-2 / TestDaF 4; English proficiency at B2/C1 for international corporate engineering dual tracks.
Research statement / Proposal
Required for Master/Doctoral candidates: corporate sponsorship endorsement and 2,000-word applied research concept aligned with industrial R&D priorities.
Application fee
EUR 0 for direct dual-study admissions.
Required documentation
Bachelor's degree certificate, detailed transcript of records, employment contract with dual partner enterprise, curriculum vitae, and employer sponsorship agreement.

Tuition fees and verified scholarship systems

Tuition is tuition-free for German and EU citizens. In Baden-Württemberg, non-EU international students pay a statutory tuition fee of EUR 1,500 per semester (EUR 3,000/year). Crucially, all dual students receive a monthly salary paid continuously by their corporate partner enterprise (ranging from EUR 1,100 to EUR 1,800 per month) throughout both academic and work phases.
