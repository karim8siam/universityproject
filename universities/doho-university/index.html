Doho University: Social Welfare, Arts & Buddhist Studies Academic Guide

Founded in 1804 as a seminary of the Shinshu Otani-ha branch of Pure Land Buddhism and chartered as a modern university in 1950, Doho University is a respected private university situated in Nakamura-ku, Nagoya. Known for its Department of Social Welfare—one of the earliest and most distinguished in central Japan—alongside creative curricula in Literature, Buddhist Studies, and Music, Doho emphasizes compassionate social contribution, human development, and community welfare.

Verified degree programs and opportunities

Review checked academic pathways and funding options below:

Doho University International Student Special Tuition Reduction Award

Tuition waiver scholarship awarded by Doho University to support international scholars pursuing social welfare, humanities, and arts education in Nagoya.

Master of Arts in Social Welfare and Clinical Human Services

Two-year research and professional training master's program preparing clinical social workers, welfare administrators, and policy researchers for certification in Japan.

Doctor of Philosophy (Ph.D.) in Buddhist Studies and Social Philosophy

Three-year doctoral program providing rigorous philological, historical, and ethical analysis of Mahayana and Shinshu Buddhist thought and its application to modern social welfare.

Aichi Regional Elderly Social Care and Community Network Field Research

Community-embedded research initiative analyzing informal elderly support networks, dementia-friendly neighborhood initiatives, and integrated community care models in central Japan.

Nagoya Municipal Healthcare and Welfare Institution Clinical Practicum

Structured 180-hour clinical fieldwork practicum embedding students in municipal hospitals, rehabilitation centers, and specialized nursing homes across Nagoya.

Doho Otani Buddhist Culture and Sacred Music Postdoctoral Fellowship

Two-year postdoctoral fellowship dedicated to documenting, archiving, and analyzing traditional Pure Land liturgical chanting (Shomyo) and sacred instrumental traditions.

Associate Professor of Child Welfare and Developmental Psychology

Tenured faculty appointment teaching undergraduate and graduate social welfare courses, supervising clinical student practica, and conducting empirical child welfare research.

Admissions, language requirements & application process

Master's admission eligibility
Graduation from a recognized undergraduate university (minimum 16 years of formal education) with a Bachelor's degree in social welfare, humanities, psychology, or related disciplines.
Doctoral admission eligibility
Master's degree in Social Welfare, Buddhist Studies, or Humanities satisfying Japanese MEXT third-cycle entrance requirements.
Language proficiency
Japanese language proficiency at JLPT N2 or N1 level required for undergraduate and graduate lecture comprehension and social casework field placements.
Research statement / Proposal
Comprehensive research proposal (1,500–2,000 characters in Japanese) outlining research background, methodology, and relevance to contemporary social welfare.
Application fee
¥35,000 non-refundable graduate application examination fee.
Required documentation
Certified bachelor's/master's degree certificate, academic transcripts, certificate of Japanese language proficiency, statement of financial support, and resident card/passport copy.

Tuition fees and verified scholarship systems

Annual tuition for undergraduate and graduate programs is approximately ¥750,000 to ¥980,000 per year, plus a one-time first-year matriculation fee of ¥200,000. Privately financed international students with valid student visas are eligible for Doho University's 30% to 50% tuition reduction scholarship based on academic performance and financial need.
