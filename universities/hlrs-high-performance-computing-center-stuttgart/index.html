University of Stuttgart - High Performance Computing Center (HLRS)

The High Performance Computing Center Stuttgart (Höchstleistungsrechenzentrum Stuttgart - HLRS) at the University of Stuttgart is one of Europe's premier national supercomputing centers, hosting the Hawk and Hunter supercomputers to power exascale science, engineering simulations, and quantum computing integration.

Academic overview & research priorities

Founded as Germany's first national supercomputing center in 1996, HLRS operates as a central research institute of the University of Stuttgart and a member of the Gauss Centre for Supercomputing (GCS). Renowned for its world-class high-performance computing (HPC) platforms, HLRS supports grand-challenge computational science across automotive aerodynamics, climate modeling, fusion energy, biomedical simulation, and exascale AI.

Research admissions & international criteria

Doctoral and research appointments at HLRS require a master's degree in computer science, mathematics, physics, or computational engineering with a distinguished record. Candidates submit a research proposal addressing parallel algorithms, GPU accelerated computing, or exascale software engineering, and are fully funded through DFG, European Horizon, or industrial partnerships.
