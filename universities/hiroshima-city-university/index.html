Hiroshima City University: Peace Studies, Information Sciences & MEXT Fellowships

Hiroshima City University (HCU / Hiroshima Shiritsu Daigaku) is a public municipal university in Hiroshima renowned for the Hiroshima Peace Institute, world-class graduate schools of Information Sciences, and fine arts. HCU bridges cutting-edge computing, peace and conflict resolution, and cultural arts.

Verified degree programs and opportunities

Review checked academic pathways and funding options below:

Japanese Government (MEXT) Embassy & University Scholarship at HCU

Comprehensive Japanese government scholarship supporting international graduate researchers in information systems, computer vision, and peace studies.

Doctor of Philosophy (PhD) in Peace Studies at Hiroshima Peace Institute (HPI)

Prestigious doctoral research based at the Hiroshima Peace Institute analyzing nuclear non-proliferation treaties, reconciliation processes, and human security in East Asia.

Master of Science in Information & Computer Engineering (Computer Vision Track)

Two-year master's curriculum exploring deep convolutional neural networks, 3D object detection, robotic visual odometry, and image restoration.

Intelligent Robotics & Human-Machine Collaboration Lab Assistantship

Funded research project developing compliant robotic arms and collaborative manipulators for industrial assembly and medical assistance.

Mazda Motor Corporation Automotive AI & Telematics Practicum

Three-month industrial practicum at Mazda headquarters in Hiroshima developing driver-assist sensor algorithms and vehicle-to-everything (V2X) communication networks.

JSPS-HCU Postdoctoral Fellowship in Nuclear Disarmament & International Security

Two-year postdoctoral fellowship supported by the Japan Society for the Promotion of Science (JSPS) based at the Hiroshima Peace Institute.

Master of Fine Arts in Traditional Japanese Painting & Contemporary Digital Media

Studio-based graduate degree bridging traditional mineral pigment Nihonga painting techniques with generative digital projection and installation arts.

Admissions, language requirements & application process

Master's admission eligibility
Completion of 16 years of schooling with a bachelor's degree in computer science, engineering, international studies, or art.
Doctoral admission eligibility
Master's degree or equivalent in a relevant field; applicants must submit an academic thesis and research plan endorsed by a prospective HCU professor.
Language proficiency
TOEFL iBT 70+, IELTS 5.5+, or Japanese JLPT N2 depending on the prospective supervisor's graduate school requirements.
Research statement / Proposal
Detailed 2–3 page research proposal outlining problem statement, methodology, and connection to HCU laboratories or the Peace Institute.
Application fee
¥30,000 JPY municipal examination fee.
Required documentation
Bachelor's/Master's certificate of graduation, academic transcript, letters of recommendation, and passport copy.

Tuition fees and verified scholarship systems

Tuition is approximately ¥535,800 JPY per year (admission fee of ¥282,000 JPY for residents of Hiroshima, ¥423,000 JPY for non-residents). International students regularly receive 50% to 100% tuition exemptions based on academic merit, and MEXT scholars receive full waivers plus living stipends.
