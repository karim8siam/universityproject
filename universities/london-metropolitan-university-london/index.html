London Metropolitan University: Computing, Architecture & Human Rights Guide

London Metropolitan University (London Met) is an energetic public research university located in central and north London, operating primary campuses in Holloway (Science, Computing, Business and Law) and Aldgate (School of Art, Architecture and Design - formerly the Sir John Cass School of Art). Dedicated to social justice, social mobility, and inclusive education, London Met features the state-of-the-art £30 million Science Centre (one of Europe's largest open-plan science teaching facilities), world-renowned cyber ranges, and award-winning architecture studios.

Admissions, language requirements & application process

Master's admission eligibility
Accredited UK Bachelor's degree (minimum 2:2 Honours or recognized international equivalent); relevant work experience considered for mature applicants through accreditation of prior learning (APL).
Doctoral admission eligibility
Recognized Master's degree in a relevant subject area with good standing; submission of a detailed research proposal and demonstration of alignment with a London Met research centre.
Language proficiency
IELTS Academic 6.0 overall (minimum 5.5 in each component) for undergraduate; 6.5 overall (minimum 6.0 in each component) for postgraduate degrees.
Research statement / Proposal
Required for doctoral applications: 2,000 to 3,000-word proposal detailing research methodology, literature review, ethical considerations, and academic originality.
Application fee
£0 for direct international online applications.
Required documentation
Academic degree certificates, official transcripts, curriculum vitae, personal statement, two reference letters, and copy of passport.

Tuition fees and verified scholarship systems

Tuition for domestic undergraduate students is £9,250 per year. International student tuition ranges from £15,000 to £18,200 per academic year. London Met awards substantial merit scholarships, including International Student Scholarships providing up to £3,000 in tuition fee reductions.
