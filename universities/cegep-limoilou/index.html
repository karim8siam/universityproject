Cégep Limoilou: Geomatics, Industrial Robotics & Engineering Technology Guide

Established in 1967 in historic Quebec City, Cégep Limoilou is one of Quebec's premier public collegiate institutes, educating over 5,000 students annually across three campuses. Renowned as a provincial center of excellence in geomatics, industrial robotics, computer engineering technology, and dietetics, Limoilou conducts leading applied research through its affiliated technology transfer centers.

Admissions, language requirements & application process

Master's admission eligibility
Quebec Diplôme d'études collégiales (DEC) or equivalent international secondary/post-secondary credential (baccalauréat de l'enseignement secondaire) with solid mathematics and physical science foundations.
Doctoral admission eligibility
Collaborative applied doctoral research coordinated in partnership with Université Laval and the Institut national de la recherche scientifique (INRS).
Language proficiency
French language proficiency at level B2/C1 (TFI, TCF, or DELF) for French-taught programs; English proficiency B2 for bilingual technology modules.
Research statement / Proposal
Required for applied research bursaries: 750-word statement of intent detailing interest in geomatics, automated manufacturing, or biomedical engineering.
Application fee
$30 CAD via Service régional d'admission au collégial de Québec (SRACQ) for domestic and international applicants.
Required documentation
Certified academic transcripts and diploma certificates with French or English translations, birth certificate, proof of French proficiency, and financial capacity declaration.

Tuition fees and verified scholarship systems

Tuition is 0 CAD for Quebec resident students (funded by the Ministère de l'Enseignement supérieur). For international candidates, tuition is approximately $14,200 to $18,500 CAD per year, with Quebec government exemption scholarships granting local Quebec tuition rates to qualifying international students.
