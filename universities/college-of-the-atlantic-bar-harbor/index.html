College of the Atlantic

College of the Atlantic is a globally acclaimed oceanfront liberal arts college in Bar Harbor on Mount Desert Island, Maine, dedicated exclusively to the interdisciplinary study of Human Ecology—the relationships between humans and their natural, social, and built environments.

Academic overview & excellence

At COA, all students design their own individualized course of study leading to a Bachelor of Arts in Human Ecology. The coastal campus encompasses two organic working farms (Beech Hill Farm, Peggy Rockefeller Farm), two offshore island research stations (Mount Desert Rock, Great Duck Island), and direct access to Acadia National Park.

Research admissions & criteria

Admissions is holistic and test-optional, seeking intellectually adventurous and environmentally engaged scholars. International candidates submit secondary transcripts, personal essays, and English proficiency (IELTS 7.0, TOEFL iBT 90, or Duolingo 115). COA meets 100% of demonstrated financial need for all admitted students, including international students.

Ten-week residential summer marine biology fellowship based at Allied Whale's Edward McC. Blair Marine Station on Mount Desert Rock, studying finback and humpback whale photo-ID catalogs.

Funding: US $3,500 research fellowship plus full offshore light station room and board Tuition: US $0 Deadline: February 15, 2027
Bachelor's

Bachelor of Arts in Human Ecology

Self-designed interdisciplinary degree integrating environmental law, marine biology, documentary filmmaking, agroecology, and green architectural design.

Funding: 100% demonstrated financial need met for all admitted students Tuition: US $45,800 per year Deadline: February 1, 2027
Internship

Beech Hill Farm Organic Agroecology & Food Systems Practicum

Fourteen-week hands-on organic farming practicum at COA's 73-acre MOFGA-certified farm, studying no-till soil microbiology, heritage crop breeding, and CSA food sovereignty.

Funding: US $16.50/hour paid summer agricultural practicum plus farm produce share Tuition: US $0 Deadline: March 31, 2027
Master's

Master of Philosophy (M.Phil.) in Human Ecology

Two-year personalized research master's degree working one-on-one with senior human ecology faculty on a major thesis in conservation biology, eco-philosophy, or environmental art.

Funding: Graduate teaching assistantships covering 50–100% of tuition and living stipend Tuition: US $26,500 total program tuition Deadline: February 15, 2027
Internship

Acadia National Park Collaborative Wildlife Management Internship

Summer research practicum tracking peregrine falcon nest success, bat echolocation surveys, and coastal wetland water quality in Acadia National Park.

Funding: US $3,200 seasonal internship stipend funded through the National Park Service partnership Tuition: US $0 Deadline: April 15, 2027
Course

Certificate in Applied Eco-Literacy & Community Solar Development

Eight-week intensive summer course covering community solar financial modeling, municipal decarbonization policy, and microgrid engineering on offshore islands.

Funding: Community climate action scholarship eligible Tuition: US $2,100 total course fee Deadline: June 30, 2027
