LSTM Liverpool: Tropical Disease Research, Global Health & PhD Guide

Established in 1898, the Liverpool School of Tropical Medicine (LSTM) is the world's first institution dedicated to tropical health research and education. An independent higher education institution with degree-awarding powers, LSTM commands an international research portfolio of over £200 million, leading global initiatives against malaria, tuberculosis, neglected tropical diseases (NTDs), and antimicrobial resistance (AMR). Partnering with the WHO, Wellcome Trust, and Gates Foundation, LSTM educates over 1,000 clinicians and public health scientists from 68 countries annually.

Admissions, language requirements & application process

Master's admission eligibility
Accredited medical degree (MBBS/MBChB) or upper second-class (2:1) Honours degree in biological sciences, nursing, pharmacy, or public health; clinical experience strongly prioritized.
Doctoral admission eligibility
First-class or upper second-class Honours degree plus relevant Master's degree (MSc or MPH with merit or distinction); accepted doctoral research proposal and supervisor agreement.
Language proficiency
IELTS Academic 6.5 overall (minimum 6.0 in all components) or 7.0 overall for clinical programs; TOEFL iBT 90 overall (minimum 21 in all skills).
Research statement / Proposal
Required for PhD and MPhil applicants: 3-5 page research proposal outlining epidemiological or molecular research questions, methodology, and public health impact.
Application fee
£0 application fee.
Required documentation
University transcripts, degree certificates, CV with publications/clinical experience, personal statement, two academic references, and passport.

Tuition fees and verified scholarship systems

Tuition for domestic postgraduate students ranges from £9,500 to £11,000 per academic year. International master's tuition ranges from £21,500 to £26,800 per year. Fully funded Wellcome Trust, Commonwealth, and Gates Foundation doctoral studentships provide full tuition, research consumables, and annual tax-free living stipends exceeding £19,000.
