North Carolina Central University

NCCU · Durham, North Carolina, United States

North Carolina Central University (NCCU) is a premier comprehensive public historically Black university (HBCU) founded in 1910, situated on a historic 135-acre campus in Durham, North Carolina, within the thriving Research Triangle. Renowned for biomedical sciences through the Julius L. Chambers Biomedical/Biotechnology Research Institute (BBRI), the Biomanufacturing Research Institute and Technology Enterprise (BRITE), the NCCU School of Law, and jazz studies, NCCU is a national beacon for biotechnology and legal equity.

Academic overview & strategic disciplines

NCCU comprises seven schools and colleges: the College of Health and Sciences, College of Arts, Social Sciences and Humanities, School of Business, School of Law, School of Library and Information Sciences (SLIS), School of Education, and the Graduate School. The university operates BBRI and the BRITE biomanufacturing pilot facility.

Research admissions & international qualifications

Undergraduate admissions evaluate high school GPA, transcripts, and personal statements with test-optional application options. Graduate doctoral and master's admissions require an accredited bachelor's degree with a minimum 2.8–3.0 GPA, statement of purpose, recommendation letters, and English proficiency (TOEFL 79+ or IELTS 6.5+). Graduate Research Assistantships (GRA) through BRITE/BBRI provide stipends up to $22,000/year plus tuition remission.
