University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa

UH Mānoa · Honolulu, Hawaii, United States

The University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa (UH Mānoa), founded in 1907 in Honolulu, is the flagship campus of the University of Hawaiʻi system and a world-class Carnegie R1 research university. Uniquely situated in the central Pacific, UH Mānoa is internationally recognized as a global capital for oceanography, marine biology, astronomy (operating telescopes atop Mauna Kea and Haleakalā), volcanology, tropical agriculture, and Pacific/Asian studies.

Academic overview & strategic disciplines

UH Mānoa encompasses the School of Ocean and Earth Science and Technology (SOEST), the Institute for Astronomy (IfA), the John A. Burns School of Medicine, the William S. Richardson School of Law, and the College of Tropical Agriculture and Human Resources (CTAHR). It commands over $400 million in annual extramural research grants.

Research admissions & international qualifications

Undergraduate admissions evaluate high school preparation via UH System application or Common App (test-optional; minimum GPA 2.8). Graduate and doctoral applicants require an accredited baccalaureate degree with a minimum 3.0 GPA, letters of recommendation, statement of purpose, and demonstrated alignment with research faculty.
