Clemson University: Academic Guide, Degrees & Scholarships

Founded in 1889 on the shores of Lake Hartwell in the foothills of the Blue Ridge Mountains, Clemson University educates more than 28,000 students. As a Carnegie R1 Very High Research Activity institution, Clemson is a powerhouse of applied science, engineering, and agriculture. The university anchors the world-renowned Clemson University International Center for Automotive Research (CU-ICAR) in Greenville, where students engineer autonomous and electric concept vehicles with BMW, Michelin, and Volvo. Clemson is also home to the Center for Optical Materials Science and Engineering Technologies (COMSET) and the Palmetto Supercomputer Cluster.
