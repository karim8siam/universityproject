Texas A&M University

Texas A&M · College Station, Texas, United States

Texas A&M University (Texas A&M / TAMU) is the flagship public land-, sea-, and space-grant Carnegie R1 doctoral research university of Texas, founded in 1876 in College Station, Texas. World-renowned for the College of Engineering, College of Agriculture & Life Sciences, School of Veterinary Medicine & Biomedical Sciences, Bush School of Government & Public Service, and Mays Business School, Texas A&M ranks among the nation's top research powerhouses with over $1.2 billion in annual research expenditures.

Academic overview & strategic disciplines

Texas A&M encompasses 16 colleges and schools, operating the Texas A&M Engineering Experiment Station (TEES), Texas A&M Transportation Institute (TTI), and Texas A&M AgriLife Research. TAMU holds the largest single-campus student body in the United States and is a founding member of the Association of American Universities (AAU).

Research admissions & international qualifications

Undergraduate admissions evaluate class rank, academic transcripts, and essays with test-optional reviews. Graduate doctoral programs offer multi-year fellowships, Graduate Research Assistantships (GRA), and Graduate Teaching Assistantships (GTA) ($28,000–$36,000/year plus full tuition remission and health insurance). Application requires transcripts, statement of purpose, three recommendation letters, and English proficiency (TOEFL 80+ or IELTS 6.5+).
