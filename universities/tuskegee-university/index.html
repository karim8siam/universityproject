Tuskegee University

Tuskegee University · Tuskegee, Alabama, United States

Tuskegee University, established in 1881 by Booker T. Washington, is one of the nation's most historic and revered Historically Black Universities (HBCUs). Located in Tuskegee, Alabama, the university is the only college campus designated as a National Historic Site. Tuskegee is globally renowned for founding the Tuskegee Airmen, pioneering agricultural discoveries by George Washington Carver, and housing the top College of Veterinary Medicine graduating over 70% of the nation's African American veterinarians.

Academic overview & strategic disciplines

Tuskegee offers undergraduate, professional, and doctoral programs through the College of Veterinary Medicine, College of Engineering, College of Agriculture, Environment and Nutrition Sciences (CAENS), College of Arts and Sciences, and Andrew F. Brimmer College of Business and Information Science. The university is an R2 doctoral research university recognized for advanced composites, aerospace materials, and nanobiotechnology.

Research admissions & international qualifications

Undergraduate admissions require high school graduation with a minimum 2.5 cumulative GPA (test-optional). Professional and graduate programs require accredited bachelor's or DVM degrees, minimum 3.0 GPA, GRE or MCAT/VMCAS scores where applicable, letters of recommendation, and personal statements.
