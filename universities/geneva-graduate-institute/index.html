Geneva Graduate Institute: Degrees, Swiss PhD Contracts & Faculty Guide

Established in 1927 alongside the League of Nations, the Geneva Graduate Institute (French: Institut de hautes études internationales et du développement - IHEID) is one of the world's most prestigious institutions dedicated exclusively to postgraduate education and research in international affairs, development economics, international law, and global governance. Located on the Maison de la Paix campus in international Geneva, IHEID maintains intimate connections with the United Nations, World Trade Organization, and WHO.

IHEID Global Excellence Full Tuition and Living Cost Scholarship

Prestigious institutional scholarship awarded on the basis of academic excellence and financial need, providing full tuition waiver and living stipends for master's candidates in Geneva.

Master in International and Development Studies (MINT)

Flagship interdisciplinary two-year master's program offering specialized tracks in conflict and peacebuilding, global environment, gender, and trade governance on the Maison de la Paix campus.

Doctor of Philosophy (Ph.D.) in Development Economics (Fully Funded)

Four-year salaried doctoral research appointment conducting rigorous theoretical and empirical investigations in international trade, development finance, and global macroeconomics.

Centre for International Environmental Studies Global Climate Governance Project

Interdisciplinary research initiative evaluating the implementation of Article 6 of the Paris Agreement, renewable energy subsidies, and transboundary water treaties.

United Nations and Geneva International Organization Curricular Practicum

Structured curricular internship embedding master's students within United Nations agencies, permanent diplomatic missions, and international human rights secretariats in Geneva.

Albert Hirschman Postdoctoral Fellowship in Democratic Governance

Two-year postdoctoral fellowship at the Albert Hirschman Centre on Democracy, supporting cutting-edge scholarship on contemporary challenges to constitutional democracy and civic freedoms.

Assistant Professor (Tenure-Track) in International Law and Global Dispute Settlement

Tenure-track faculty position teaching graduate public international law, conducting research on international adjudication and arbitration, and supervising doctoral dissertations.

Admissions, language requirements & application process

Master's admission eligibility
Accredited Bachelor's degree (minimum 180 ECTS credits) or recognized international equivalent in a relevant social science, economics, law, or humanities discipline with high academic distinction.
Doctoral admission eligibility
Recognized Master's degree (minimum 60–120 ECTS) in international relations, economics, law, or history with outstanding academic marks and an approved doctoral research proposal.
Language proficiency
Proficiency in English at CEFR C1 level (IELTS Academic 7.0 overall with no band below 6.5, or TOEFL iBT 100); passive knowledge of French is advantageous but not mandatory.
Research statement / Proposal
Online application via the IHEID portal including two academic letters of reference, personal statement of purpose, sample of written scholarly work, and CV.
Application fee
CHF 100 non-refundable application evaluation fee.
Required documentation
Certified university diplomas, complete official transcripts with grading scales, resume/CV, language certificates, and financial assistance application form if requesting aid.

Tuition fees and verified scholarship systems

Tuition for Master's programs is CHF 2,500 per semester for Swiss domestic students and CHF 4,000 per semester for non-resident international students (CHF 8,000 per academic year). Employed doctoral assistants receive competitive Swiss National Science Foundation (SNSF) salaries ranging from CHF 48,000 to CHF 55,000 per annum with zero tuition fees. Approximately 50% of master's students receive full or partial institutional scholarships based on financial need and academic excellence.
