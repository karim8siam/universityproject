Baruch College CUNY: Zicklin MBA, Quantitative Finance & Public Affairs Guide

Bernard M. Baruch College of the City University of New York (CUNY) is an elite public institution located in the Gramercy and Flatiron districts of Manhattan, New York City. Consistently ranked #1 in the United States for social mobility and among the top public business schools nationwide, Baruch is home to the world-renowned Zicklin School of Business (the largest collegiate school of business in the US), the Austin W. Marxe School of Public and International Affairs, and the Weissman School of Arts and Sciences.

Admissions, language requirements & application process

Master's admission eligibility
Accredited four-year U.S. bachelor's degree or recognized international equivalent with a minimum 3.0 GPA; quantitative proficiency required for business analytics, statistics, and finance degrees; GMAT/GRE may be waived for qualified applicants.
Doctoral admission eligibility
Conferred jointly through the CUNY Graduate Center; requires superior undergraduate and master's record, competitive GRE/GMAT, and proven research aptitude in business or economics.
Language proficiency
TOEFL iBT 100 overall (minimum 22 in all subscores) or IELTS Academic 7.0 overall (minimum 6.5 in all bands) for international graduates from non-English speaking institutions.
Research statement / Proposal
Required for graduate applicants: 500-1,000 word statement of purpose outlining professional goals and rationale for choosing Baruch in New York City.
Application fee
$75 USD application fee for graduate degree programs.
Required documentation
Official academic transcripts, certified English translations, resume/curriculum vitae, two letters of recommendation, standardized test scores (if applicable), and personal essay.

Tuition fees and verified scholarship systems

Tuition for New York State residents averages $5,530 to $7,700 per semester for graduate programs. Non-resident and international graduate tuition is approximately $855 to $1,050 per credit hour (averaging $21,000 to $28,000 per academic year). Baruch awards substantial merit fellowships, Zicklin Dean's Scholarships, and graduate assistantships.
