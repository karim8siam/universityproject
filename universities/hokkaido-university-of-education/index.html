Hokkaido University of Education: Teacher Pedagogy, Special Needs & Science Education Guide

Established in 1949 as a prestigious national university of Japan, Hokkaido University of Education (Hokkyodai) operates five specialized campuses across Hokkaido (Sapporo, Asahikawa, Hakodate, Kushiro, and Iwamizawa). As northern Japan's flagship teacher-training and pedagogical research institution, Hokkyodai excels in educational psychology, special needs education, comparative international pedagogy, environmental sciences, and fine arts.

Japanese Government (MEXT) National Teacher Training Scholarship at Hokkyodai

Prestigious Monbukagakusho (MEXT) national scholarship providing full financial support to international educators conducting graduate-level educational research in Japan.

Master of Education in Special Needs & Developmental Psychology

Two-year professional master's degree training pedagogical specialists and clinical educators in autism spectrum interventions, learning disabilities, and inclusive classroom design.

United Graduate School of Education Collaborative Doctoral Fellowship

Three-year doctoral research fellowship investigating international comparative curriculum standards, science education epistemologies, and teacher professional growth.

Hokkaido Northern Environment & Indigenous Ainu Cultural Education Project

Funded ethnological research project exploring place-based ecological education, indigenous language revitalisation, and multicultural curriculum development across Hokkaido.

Hokkaido Attached Laboratory Schools Clinical Teaching Practicum

Four-month intensive clinical teaching practicum embedded within Hokkyodai's affiliated elementary, junior high, and special needs schools executing experimental pedagogies.

Hokkyodai Senior Postdoctoral Fellow in Science Education Pedagogy

Two-year postdoctoral research appointment developing hands-on experimental inquiry modules and computational thinking curriculum frameworks for secondary physics education.

Hokkyodai International Exchange and Student Support Association Award

Institutional scholarship granted to international students who combine academic diligence with active engagement in local school intercultural exchange programs.

Admissions, language requirements & application process

Master's admission eligibility
Accredited Bachelor's degree (16 years of school education) from a recognized university with satisfactory academic standing in education, sciences, or humanities.
Doctoral admission eligibility
Direct collaborative doctoral programs facilitated through the United Graduate School of Education in consortium with Japanese national universities.
Language proficiency
Japanese Language Proficiency Test (JLPT) N2 or N1 for Japanese-taught tracks; TOEFL iBT 80 or IELTS 6.0 for international comparative education research tracks.
Research statement / Proposal
Required for graduate applicants: 2,000-character Japanese or 1,200-word English research proposal (Kenkyu Keikakusho) in educational methodology or developmental psychology.
Application fee
¥30,000 JPY national university graduate admission screening fee.
Required documentation
Graduation certificate and academic transcripts, JLPT/TOEFL scorecard, research proposal, two letters of recommendation, and financial statement.

Tuition fees and verified scholarship systems

As a national public university of Japan, tuition is strictly standardized at ¥535,800 JPY per year (plus a one-time admission fee of ¥282,000 JPY). International students can apply for 50% or 100% national university tuition waivers based on academic merit, and MEXT scholars receive full tuition exemption and living stipends.
