Washburn University

Washburn · Topeka, Kansas, United States

Washburn University is a historic public municipal university founded in 1865 on a 160-acre campus in Topeka, the capital city of Kansas. Famous for the Washburn University School of Law—where landmark constitutional jurisprudence including Brown v. Board of Education was argued—Washburn is also celebrated for state-of-the-art forensic science at the KBI Forensic Science Center, nursing, and business.

Academic overview & strategic disciplines

Washburn comprises six academic units: the College of Arts and Sciences, Washburn University School of Law, School of Business, School of Nursing, School of Applied Studies, and the Washburn Institute of Technology. The campus hosts the Kansas Bureau of Investigation (KBI) Forensic Science Center.

Research admissions & international qualifications

Undergraduate admissions evaluate high school GPA (minimum 2.5) and coursework. Graduate admissions require an accredited bachelor's degree with a minimum 2.75–3.0 GPA, statement of purpose, two to three letters of recommendation, and English language proficiency (TOEFL 79+ or IELTS 6.5+).
