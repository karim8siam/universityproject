Teologiska Högskolan Stockholm: Degrees, Scholarships & Research Guide

Located in Bromma, Stockholm, Teologiska Högskolan Stockholm (Enskilda Högskolan Stockholm - EHS) is an accredited Swedish higher education institution recognized nationally for theology, human rights, democracy, and international peace studies. EHS operates specialized graduate institutes with strong connections to European non-governmental organizations.

Swedish Institute Scholarship for Global Professionals (SISGP) at EHS

Prestigious Swedish government scholarship funding full tuition, healthcare insurance, and monthly living costs for future global leaders.

Master of Arts in International Human Rights and Democracy

Rigorous international master's curriculum exploring international humanitarian law, transitional justice, indigenous rights, and democratic institutional design.

EHS Salaried Doctoral Research Position in Human Rights & Ethics

Four-year salaried doctoral research fellowship investigating international human rights frameworks, religious freedom, and democratic resilience.

EHS Center for Human Rights and Peacebuilding Research Project

Collaborative research project analyzing migration governance, human rights compliance, and civil society peacebuilding mechanisms across Scandinavia.

Stockholm NGO & Civil Society Human Rights Practicum

One-semester practical placement embedded within Swedish and international human rights organizations, civil rights ombudsmen, and intergovernmental bodies.

EHS Postdoctoral Research Fellowship in Comparative Ethics and Global Justice

Two-year postdoctoral fellowship conducting research on distributive justice, cosmopolitan ethics, and institutional accountability in international relations.

Universitetslektor (Senior Lecturer) in Human Rights Studies

Permanent faculty appointment responsible for delivering master's level lectures in international human rights protection and supervising postgraduate theses.

Admissions, language requirements & application process

Master's admission eligibility
Completed Bachelor of Arts or Bachelor of Science degree in human rights, law, political science, sociology, or theology (minimum 180 ECTS credits).
Doctoral admission eligibility
Recognized Master's degree (minimum 120 ECTS credits) including an independent research thesis of at least 15 ECTS credits.
Language proficiency
Proficiency in English equivalent to Swedish upper secondary course English 6 (IELTS Academic 6.5 with no band below 5.5, or TOEFL iBT 90).
Research statement / Proposal
Statement of purpose (approx 1,000 words) discussing motivation and specific human rights or theological research questions.
Application fee
SEK 900 centralized application fee for non-EU/EEA students applying through University Admissions Sweden.
Required documentation
Certificates of completed higher education degrees, academic transcripts with grading key, proof of English language proficiency, and copy of passport.

Tuition fees and verified scholarship systems

Under Swedish higher education law, citizens of Sweden and EU/EEA/Switzerland study 100% tuition-free. For non-EU/EEA international students, tuition is approximately SEK 60,000 per semester (SEK 120,000 per year / SEK 240,000 for a 2-year 120-credit Master's degree). Swedish Institute Scholarships cover full tuition plus monthly living expenses for qualifying international students.
