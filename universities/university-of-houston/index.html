University of Houston: Energy Engineering, Superconductivity & Computer Science

The University of Houston (UH), founded in 1927, is a premier Tier-One public research university (Carnegie R1) situated in the energy capital of the world, home to the Texas Center for Superconductivity (TcSUH), world-leading petroleum and chemical engineering, and the Cullen College of Engineering.

Verified degree programs and opportunities

Review checked academic pathways and funding options below:

UH Presidential Graduate Fellowship & Graduate Tuition Fellowship (GTF)

Premier merit-based doctoral award granted to exceptional incoming PhD students across Cullen College of Engineering, College of Natural Sciences and Mathematics, and Bauer College of Business.

Master of Science in Subsea & Energy Systems Engineering

Pioneering engineering master's program located in Houston, focusing on offshore pipeline engineering, subsea robotics, carbon capture and storage (CCS), and offshore hydrogen pipelines.

Ph.D. in High-Temperature Superconductivity & Quantum Materials Fellowship

Doctoral research position at the world-famous Texas Center for Superconductivity (TcSUH) synthesizing high-Tc cuprate and nickelate superconductors for zero-loss power transmission.

Gulf Coast Hydrogen Hub & Industrial Decarbonization Research Project

Interdisciplinary clean energy project at UH Energy modeling regional hydrogen pipelines, salt cavern geological storage, and industrial carbon capture in the Houston Ship Channel.

Houston Energy Corridor & NASA Johnson Space Center Curricular Practicum

Four-month paid engineering co-op placement stationed in the Houston Energy Corridor (ExxonMobil, Chevron, SLB) or at NASA Johnson Space Center contractors.

TcSUH Postdoctoral Research Fellowship in Superconducting Magnet Devices

Two-year postdoctoral research appointment developing high-field superconducting magnets and superconducting magnetic energy storage (SMES) for fusion reactors and MRI.

Tenure-Track Assistant Professor in Computer Science & Cloud Security

Full-time tenure-track faculty position in the Department of Computer Science, leading research in distributed cloud systems, confidential computing, and zero-trust security.

Admissions, language requirements & application process

Master's admission eligibility
Earned Bachelor's degree from an accredited institution with minimum 3.0 cumulative GPA on a 4.0 scale.
Doctoral admission eligibility
Earned Master's or outstanding Bachelor's degree with minimum 3.4 GPA and strong research aptitude in STEM or business fields.
Language proficiency
TOEFL iBT 79 overall (minimum 20 in each section) or IELTS Academic 6.5 overall.
Research statement / Proposal
Doctoral statement of research intent (1,000 words) identifying specific research faculty and laboratories at UH.
Application fee
$75 domestic / $140 international graduate application fee.
Required documentation
Official university transcripts, statement of purpose, resume/CV, and three letters of recommendation.

Tuition fees and verified scholarship systems

In-state graduate tuition averages $9,400 to $11,200 annually. Out-of-state and international graduate tuition is approximately $21,500 to $24,800 annually. Doctoral students in engineering and sciences typically receive full tuition fellowships (GTF) plus annual assistantship stipends ($24,000–$34,000).
