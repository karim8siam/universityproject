Laurentian University: Mining Engineering, SNOLAB Physics & Graduate Funding

Laurentian University (Université Laurentienne) is a premier bilingual and tricultural research university in Greater Sudbury, Ontario, renowned worldwide for mining engineering, mineral exploration, environmental restoration (regreening), and world-leading astroparticle physics partnerships at SNOLAB.

Verified degree programs and opportunities

Review checked academic pathways and funding options below:

Laurentian Graduate Teaching & Research Fellowship (GTA/GRA)

Competitive graduate assistantship support for incoming full-time master's and PhD students across engineering, science, and environmental disciplines.

Doctor of Philosophy (PhD) in Boreal Ecology & Environmental Remediation

Doctoral research examining boreal forest restoration, mine tailings revegetation, watershed recovery, and heavy metal bioaccumulation in northern biomes.

Master of Science (MSc) in Mineral Exploration & Economic Geology

World-class economic geology master's based in Sudbury's nickel-copper-PGE mining district, exploring ore genesis, 3D structural modeling, and drillcore geochemistry.

SNOLAB Deep Underground Astroparticle Physics Research Assistantship

Graduate research based at SNOLAB, the world's deepest cleanroom laboratory located 2 km underground in Vale's Creighton Mine, studying dark matter and neutrino properties.

Mining Automation & Tele-Robotics Industrial Practicum

Cooperative industry practicum with Sudbury mining majors and autonomous equipment makers working on battery electric vehicles (BEVs) and tele-operated scoops.

Centre for Research in Occupational Safety & Health (CROSH) Postdoctoral Fellowship

Two-year postdoctoral fellowship at CROSH evaluating whole-body vibration, mental fatigue in extreme industrial environments, and wearable biomechanical monitors.

Master of Engineering (MEng) in Fast-Track Mineral Processing & Automation

Course-based professional master's degree training process engineers in hydrometallurgy, froth flotation, smelter emissions reduction, and process automation.

Admissions, language requirements & application process

Master's admission eligibility
Four-year bachelor's degree from a recognized university with minimum 70% to 75% average (B standing) over the final two years of study.
Doctoral admission eligibility
Master's degree with minimum 75% (B+) average and demonstrated capacity for original scientific or scholarly research.
Language proficiency
IELTS Academic 6.5 (no band below 6.0) or TOEFL iBT 88 (minimum 20 in each section); French DALF C1 for French-language streams.
Research statement / Proposal
For research master's and doctoral degrees: 2–3 page statement of research intent aligning with faculty labs and mining/physics facilities.
Application fee
$120 CAD institutional application fee through the Laurentian Graduate Admissions Portal.
Required documentation
Official academic transcripts, 2–3 academic reference letters, curriculum vitae, and statement of research interests.

Tuition fees and verified scholarship systems

International graduate tuition at Laurentian is approximately $22,800 to $26,400 CAD per year. Thesis-stream graduate students receive financial support packages combining Graduate Teaching Assistantships (GTA) paying ~$11,000 CAD/year and Graduate Research Assistantships (GRA).
