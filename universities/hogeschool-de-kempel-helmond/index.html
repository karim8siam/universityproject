Hogeschool de Kempel: Primary Teacher Education, Pedagogical Innovation Guide

Hogeschool de Kempel is an internationally celebrated specialist university of applied sciences situated in Helmond, North Brabant, Netherlands. Consistently ranked the #1 teacher training institute (PABO - Pedagogische Academie voor het Basisonderwijs) in the Netherlands by national student satisfaction surveys (NSE) and Keuzegids Hbo, De Kempel educates future educators through innovative pedagogical models, neuroscience in early childhood education, educational technology integration, and inclusive classroom practices.

Admissions, language requirements & application process

Master's admission eligibility
Master of Educational Needs and Pedagogical Leadership requires an accredited Bachelor of Education (PABO) or equivalent first-cycle teaching degree, plus a minimum of two years of classroom teaching experience.
Doctoral admission eligibility
Not applicable; independent specialized university of applied sciences focusing on Bachelor of Education (B.Ed.) and Master of Education (M.Ed.) degrees.
Language proficiency
Dutch language proficiency (NT2 State Examination Program II) mandatory for standard teacher certification in the Netherlands; English B2 for international guest seminars and Erasmus+ exchange modules.
Research statement / Proposal
Required for Master's applicants: professional development plan and school-based practice inquiry proposal (500-1,000 words).
Application fee
€0 direct application fee via Studielink.nl.
Required documentation
Secondary school diploma (HAVO, VWO, or international equivalent), national PABO entrance test results (geography, history, nature/technology for non-VWO holders), CV, and ID.

Tuition fees and verified scholarship systems

Tuition is the statutory Dutch government fee of approximately €2,530 per academic year for Dutch and EU/EEA students. Institutional tuition for non-EEA students is approximately €8,500 to €9,500 per year. First-year Dutch teacher education students historically benefit from Dutch government tuition reductions.
