Academic overview & excellence

Established in 1982 by international law visionary Antonio Papisca at the historic University of Padua (founded 1222), this prestigious university centre coordinates the European Master's Programme in Human Rights and Democratisation (EMA/Global Campus of Human Rights), the UNESCO Chair in Human Rights, Democracy and Peace, and the Italian Human Rights Yearbook. The centre trains diplomatic delegates, UN human rights officers, and international NGO advocates.

Research admissions & criteria

Candidates for the Master of Arts in Human Rights and Multi-level Governance must hold an accredited bachelor's degree (minimum 180 ECTS) in law, political science, sociology, or international relations with a minimum grade equivalent to 3.0/4.0. Fluent English (C1/IELTS 7.0) is mandatory. Doctoral fellowship candidates in Human Rights, Society, and Multi-level Governance apply through the international competitive selection of the University of Padua.
