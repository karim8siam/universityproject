Beloit College: Anthropology, Environmental Biology & Economics Guide

Founded in 1846, Beloit College is an acclaimed independent liberal arts college situated on a 40-acre wooded campus along the Rock River in Beloit, southern Wisconsin. Recognized nationally for undergraduate teaching innovation and revolutionary Career Channels, Beloit combines rigorous cross-disciplinary academics with hands-on museum curation at its world-renowned Logan Museum of Anthropology and Wright Museum of Art. Its sustainable, student-centered campus features The Powerhouse—an award-winning student student union and recreation facility converted from a historic riverside power station.

Admissions, language requirements & application process

Master's admission eligibility
Not applicable; undergraduate-focused liberal arts college offering Bachelor of Arts and Bachelor of Science degrees with pre-professional dual-degree partnerships.
Doctoral admission eligibility
Not applicable; focus on undergraduate research mentorship preparing students for top doctoral and medical programs across the US.
Language proficiency
TOEFL iBT 80 overall, IELTS Academic 6.5 overall, or Duolingo English Test (DET) 110+ for international applicants.
Research statement / Proposal
Common Application personal essay; supplemental short answers demonstrating curiosity, interdisciplinary interest, and community engagement.
Application fee
$0 USD application fee via Common Application.
Required documentation
Official secondary school transcripts, certified English translation, secondary school report with counselor recommendation, one teacher recommendation, and English proficiency score.

Tuition fees and verified scholarship systems

Tuition is approximately $58,200 USD per academic year (plus $11,400 room and board). Beloit provides generous merit-based scholarships up to $44,000 USD per year to international and domestic applicants, making attendance accessible regardless of family background.
