College of the North Atlantic: Marine Tech, Geomatics & Clean Energy Guide

Established in 1997 through the consolidation of public colleges, College of the North Atlantic (CNA) is Newfoundland and Labrador's premier public college system operating 17 campuses. Renowned across Atlantic Canada for its Office of Applied Research and Innovation (OARI), CNA leads pioneering applied research in offshore green hydrogen, marine autonomous systems, precision geomatics, and mining automation.

Verified degree programs and opportunities

Review checked academic pathways and funding options below:

CNA Applied Research & Innovation Fellowship in Offshore Wind and Hydrogen

Competitive fellowship at the CNA Energy Transition Centre supporting emerging engineers and researchers designing renewable energy storage and marine hydrogen technologies.

Post-Graduate Certificate in Geomatics and Geographic Information Systems

One-year intensive postgraduate credential delivering advanced skills in satellite remote sensing, lidar point-cloud processing, drone photogrammetry, and spatial database design.

Joint Memorial-CNA Doctoral Fellowship in Autonomous Marine Navigation

Four-year joint doctoral research appointment investigating autonomous vessel path planning, obstacle avoidance in harsh North Atlantic ice environments, and underwater acoustic telemetry.

Newfoundland Boreal Forest Biomass & Mineral Spectral Analysis Project

Applied research project utilizing drone-mounted hyperspectral imaging and AI classification to map critical mineral outcroppings and quantify boreal carbon stocks across Newfoundland.

Atlantic Canada Marine Autonomous Robotics & ROV Field Internship

Sixteen-week industrial placement with ocean technology firms operating remotely operated vehicles (ROV) and subsea sensors for offshore oil, gas, and renewable installations.

CNA Postdoctoral Industrial Fellowship in Hydrogen Fuel Cell Testing

Two-year postdoctoral research appointment at the Stephenville Hydrogen Research Centre evaluating high-durability polymer electrolyte membranes under Atlantic climatic conditions.

Newfoundland and Labrador Global Academic Excellence Entrance Award

Merit award sponsored by the College of the North Atlantic Foundation supporting gifted international students enrolling in computer systems, robotics, and engineering technologies.

Admissions, language requirements & application process

Master's admission eligibility
Accredited Bachelor of Technology, Bachelor of Engineering, or recognized undergraduate degree with minimum 65%+ or GPA 3.0/4.0 in pertinent discipline.
Doctoral admission eligibility
Collaborative applied doctoral research appointments organized jointly with Memorial University of Newfoundland (MUN) and national research institutes.
Language proficiency
IELTS Academic 6.5 overall (no band below 6.0), TOEFL iBT 84 (minimum 21 all bands), or Pearson PTE Academic 58+.
Research statement / Proposal
Required for advanced applied research fellowships: 1,500-word applied research proposal detailing industrial partner collaboration and prototype development scope.
Application fee
$50 CAD domestic application fee; $100 CAD for international applicants.
Required documentation
Official post-secondary transcripts and diploma certificates, curriculum vitae, two letters of academic or industrial recommendation, and proof of English proficiency.

Tuition fees and verified scholarship systems

Tuition at College of the North Atlantic is approximately $3,600 CAD per semester for Canadian domestic students and $7,200 CAD per semester ($14,400 CAD/year) for international candidates. Comprehensive applied research assistantships, Atlantic Canada Innovation grants, and industry sponsorships offer robust financial backing.
