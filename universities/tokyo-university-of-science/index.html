Tokyo University of Science: Degrees, Scholarships & Faculty Guide

Tokyo University of Science (TUS; Tokyo Rika Daigaku), founded in 1881 by 21 physics graduates from the University of Tokyo, is one of Japan's oldest and most prestigious private STEM universities. Celebrated as the alma mater of Nobel Laureate Satoshi Ōmura, TUS is internationally acclaimed for pioneering titanium dioxide photocatalysis, supramolecular chemistry, quantum materials, and robotics.

Verified degree programs and opportunities

Review checked academic pathways and funding options below:

Doctoral Program in Pure and Applied Chemistry (Photocatalysis, Solar Energy Conversion & Supramolecular Materials)

Three-year doctoral candidacy in the Department of Pure and Applied Chemistry, investigating solar water splitting, photocatalytic air purification, and self-assembling metal-organic supramolecular cages.

Master of Science in Quantum Physics and Condensed Matter Nanotechnology

Two-year master's research curriculum in low-temperature physics, exploring high-Tc superconductivity mechanisms, topological insulators, and semiconductor spintronics devices.

Satoshi Ōmura Memorial Doctoral Scholarship for Science and Engineering Pioneers

Prestigious doctoral scholarship established in honour of Nobel laureate Satoshi Ōmura (TUS alumnus), awarded to top-tier PhD candidates demonstrating revolutionary potential in science and engineering.

Research Institute for Biomedical Sciences (RIBS) Immunology and Organoid Biology Fellowship

Research appointment at the Noda Campus RIBS center developing human intestinal organoids, analyzing T-cell checkpoint regulation, and profiling immune microenvironments in oncology models.

TUS Photocatalysis International Research Center Summer Research Practicum in AI for Materials Discovery

Eight-week research internship applying graph neural networks and density functional theory (DFT) high-throughput screening to discover novel oxide photocatalysts for hydrogen generation.

JSPS / TUS International Postdoctoral Fellowship in Applied Mathematical Physics and Non-linear Dynamics

Two-year postdoctoral fellowship administered jointly with the Japan Society for the Promotion of Science (JSPS), exploring integrable systems, soliton theory, and non-linear partial differential equations.

Master of Engineering in Advanced Mechanical Engineering and Space Systems Robotics

Two-year graduate engineering program investigating lunar exploration rovers, multi-limbed walking robotics, deployable space structures, and satellite attitude control algorithms.

Admissions, language requirements & application process

Master's admission eligibility
Completion of 16 years of formal education leading to a recognized bachelor's degree in a physical science or engineering discipline.
Doctoral admission eligibility
A recognized master's degree or professional degree; submission of prior academic thesis, research proposal, and acceptance from a prospective TUS supervisor.
Language proficiency
Proficiency in English (TOEFL iBT 80 or IELTS 6.5 recommended) or Japanese (JLPT N2/N1 for Japanese-medium graduate divisions).
Research statement / Proposal
A 3-5 page research outline including specific problem formulation, academic rationale, experimental apparatus required, and target completion schedule.
Application fee
¥35,000 examination screening fee.
Required documentation
Graduation certificates, academic transcripts, master's thesis abstract, CV, 2 confidential letters of recommendation, and supervisor pre-approval letter.

Tuition fees and verified scholarship systems

Graduate tuition at Tokyo University of Science averages ¥900,000 to ¥1,200,000 per year (approximately USD $6,000 to $8,000). Highly competitive international applicants are eligible for the Japanese Government (MEXT) University Recommendation Scholarship, which provides a 100% full tuition exemption, round-trip international flights, and a monthly tax-free living stipend of ¥145,000 to ¥148,000.
