Dacapo Mariestad: Degree Programs, Scholarships & Research Guide

Located on Lake Vänern in Mariestad, Dacapo Mariestad is Sweden's renowned higher education and research center for traditional building conservation, sustainable craft science, and landscape architecture, operated in close academic partnership with the University of Gothenburg. It pioneers ecological building crafts, historic masonry restoration, and garden conservation.

Verified degree programs and opportunities

Review checked academic pathways and funding options below:

Västra Götaland Heritage Craft Preservation Fellowship

Tuition support fellowship awarded to incoming students entering specialized traditional building conservation and historic landscape restoration programs.

Master in Conservation and Traditional Building Crafts (with Gothenburg University)

Two-year master's curriculum conducted in Mariestad's dedicated workshops exploring historic lime mortars, traditional log timbering, historic roofing, and heritage ethics.

Craft Sciences & Traditional Material Degradation Doctoral Fellowship

Four-year salaried doctoral research fellowship in craft science exploring tacit artisanal knowledge, historical timber decay, and ecological preservation methodologies.

Dacapo Historic Gardens & Heirloom Plant Conservation Project

Field research project conducted in historic West Swedish estates documenting 18th-century gardening techniques, historical orchard preservation, and heritage cultivar propagation.

National Heritage Board (Riksantikvarieämbetet) Restoration Practicum

Sixteen weeks of hands-on workplace restoration training working on medieval churches, historic manor houses, and timber monuments across southern Sweden.

Dacapo Postdoctoral Research Fellowship in Bio-Based Building Conservation Materials

Two-year postdoctoral fellowship testing vapor-permeable insulation, linseed oil binders, and bio-based coatings for energy retrofit of historical buildings.

Universitetslektor (Senior Lecturer) in Traditional Building Crafts & Architecture

Permanent senior faculty position leading studio and workshop instruction in timber construction, historic joinery, and conservation theory.

Admissions, language requirements & application process

Master's admission eligibility
Accredited Bachelor's degree (minimum 180 ECTS credits) in conservation science, architecture, landscape planning, or craft sciences.
Doctoral admission eligibility
Recognized Master's degree (minimum 120 ECTS credits) in craft science, conservation, or cultural heritage satisfying Swedish third-cycle requirements.
Language proficiency
Swedish upper secondary school English 6 (IELTS Academic 6.5) and Swedish 3/TISUS for Swedish-medium craft workshops.
Research statement / Proposal
Statement of intent detailing conservation craft background, portfolio of restoration projects, and academic CV.
Application fee
SEK 900 for non-EU/EEA students applying through University Admissions Sweden (SEK 0 for Swedish YH portals).
Required documentation
Certified degree certificates, official transcripts with grading rubric, portfolio of practical work, and copy of passport.

Tuition fees and verified scholarship systems

Under Swedish higher education regulations, Swedish, EU, EEA, and Swiss citizens study 100% tuition-free and are eligible for state CSN study allowances and loans. Non-EU/EEA international students pay tuition between SEK 65,000 and SEK 90,000 per academic year depending on the craft workshop and university partner regulations.
