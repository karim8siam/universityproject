Yukon University

Yukon University, headquartered at the Ayamdigut/Dena Cho Ke'ndi Campus in Whitehorse, is Canada's only university north of 60, renowned for pioneering circumpolar studies, permafrost geotechnical engineering, indigenous self-governance, and climate change adaptation.

Academic overview & excellence

YukonU offers specialized certificate, diploma, bachelor's, and collaborative post-graduate pathways created in formal collaboration with all 14 Yukon First Nations. The campus hosts the Yukon Research Centre, featuring cold-climate innovation labs and permafrost testing facilities.

Research admissions & criteria

Admission requires high school graduation with Grade 12 English and appropriate quantitative electives (minimum 65-70% overall average). Applicants must show English proficiency (IELTS 6.0 with no band under 6.0, or Duolingo 105). Subsidized on-campus student housing is available overlooking the Yukon River valley.

Competitive scholarship for full-time scholars enrolled in northern science, climate adaptation, or First Nations governance degrees.

Funding: CAD $5,000 annual educational bursary plus Arctic field travel grant Tuition: CAD $4,400/year (domestic standard), CAD $14,200/year (international) Deadline: February 15, 2027
Bachelor's

Bachelor of Arts in Indigenous Governance

Unique four-year degree examining modern land claim implementation, self-government agreements, indigenous legal traditions, and circumpolar political structures.

Funding: First Nation post-secondary education program funding eligible Tuition: CAD $4,500 per academic year Deadline: April 15, 2027
Fellowship

Permafrost Geotechnical Engineering & Climate Adaptation Fellowship

Twelve-month postgraduate fellowship monitoring permafrost thaw infrastructure hazards along the Alaska Highway and northern transport corridors.

Funding: CAD $22,000 research fellowship funded through the Yukon Research Centre Tuition: CAD $0 Deadline: March 31, 2027
Internship

Kluane Lake Field Station Glaciology & Ecology Field Practicum

Three-month summer field practicum based at Kluane National Park measuring St. Elias glacier retreat, boreal forest phenology, and caribou herd migration.

Funding: CAD $2,600 monthly living stipend and subsidized field camp accommodation Tuition: CAD $0 Deadline: May 1, 2027
Bachelor's

Diploma in Northern Environmental and Conservation Sciences (ENCS)

Two-year scientific diploma transferring directly into the University of Alberta BSc in Environmental and Conservation Sciences, taught entirely in Whitehorse.

Funding: Transferable dual-degree scholarships in partnership with University of Alberta Tuition: CAD $4,400 per academic year Deadline: May 30, 2027
Master's

Master of Science in Circumpolar Studies (Joint Degree)

Multilateral postgraduate curriculum exploring circumpolar geopolitics, northern natural resource economies, and climate resilience across the Arctic Council member nations.

Funding: UArctic graduate mobility grants: up to CAD $8,000 Tuition: CAD $7,500 total program tuition Deadline: February 28, 2027
Course

Certificate in Northern Mine Remediation & Water Quality Monitoring

Eight-month intensive practical course covering acid rock drainage prevention, cold-climate bioleaching treatment, and automated water sampling instrumentation.

Funding: Mining Association of Canada educational bursaries Tuition: CAD $3,100 total program fee Deadline: June 30, 2027
