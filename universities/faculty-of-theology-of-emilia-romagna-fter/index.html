FTER Bologna: Baccalaureate, Licentiate & Doctorate in Sacred Theology Guide

The Faculty of Theology of Emilia Romagna (Facoltà Teologica dell'Emilia-Romagna / FTER) is an ecclesiastical university institution canonically erected by the Holy See's Dicastery for Culture and Education, located in the historic centre of Bologna. FTER delivers accredited canonical degrees—the Baccalaureate (STB), Licentiate (STL), and Doctorate (STD) in Sacred Theology—with distinctive specializations in Evangelization, Interreligious Dialogue, and Christian History. Its library and research centres collaborate closely with the University of Bologna and European theological faculties.

Admissions, language requirements & application process

Master's admission eligibility
Ecclesiastical Licentiate (STL) requires an accredited Baccalaureate in Sacred Theology (STB) with a minimum grade of Magna cum Laude (8.5/10), or recognized second-cycle theological equivalent.
Doctoral admission eligibility
Ecclesiastical Doctorate (STD) requires an STL with exceptional academic standing (Summa cum Laude), proven fluency in classical biblical languages (Hebrew, Greek, Latin), and acceptance by a doctoral dissertation director.
Language proficiency
Italian language proficiency at B2/C1 level for academic coursework; reading competence in Latin and ancient Greek mandatory for canonical theological degrees.
Research statement / Proposal
Required for doctoral admission: 10-15 page preliminary dissertation schema including hermeneutical rationale, source bibliography, and methodological approach.
Application fee
€50 matriculation fee.
Required documentation
Ecclesiastical academic transcripts, canonical degrees, letter of presentation from religious ordinary or diocesan bishop (or academic dean), curriculum vitae, and passport.

Tuition fees and verified scholarship systems

Tuition is highly subsidized by ecclesiastical endowments and the Italian Episcopal Conference (CEI), averaging €850 to €1,400 per academic year. Generous CEI scholarships and FTER study grants are available for diocesan seminarians, religious sisters, and international lay students.
