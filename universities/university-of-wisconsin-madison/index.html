University of Wisconsin–Madison

UW–Madison · Madison, Wisconsin, United States

The University of Wisconsin–Madison, founded in 1848 as the state's flagship land-grant institution, is a world-class Carnegie R1 research university situated on the shores of Lake Mendota in Madison, Wisconsin. Celebrated for the 'Wisconsin Idea'—the principle that university research should improve lives across the globe—UW–Madison is internationally renowned for stem cell discovery, limnology, computer sciences, biochemistry, and agricultural innovation.

Academic overview & strategic disciplines

UW–Madison spans 13 schools and colleges, including the College of Letters & Science, College of Engineering, School of Medicine and Public Health, and the School of Computer, Data & Information Sciences (CDIS). With over $1.5 billion in annual research expenditures, the university houses the Wisconsin Institute for Discovery, the Center for Limnology, and the McArdle Laboratory for Cancer Research.

Research admissions & international qualifications

Undergraduate admissions are highly competitive through the Common Application (holistic review; middle 50% GPA 3.8–4.0; test-optional). Graduate and doctoral admissions require an accredited bachelor's degree, strong GPA (minimum 3.0–3.5 depending on program), letters of recommendation, and research statements.
