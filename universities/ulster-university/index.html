Ulster University: Degree Programs, Scholarships & Research Guide

With its landmark £368 million city-centre campus in Belfast alongside campuses in Coleraine and Derry~Londonderry, Ulster University is Northern Ireland's leading civic research institution. Ranked top 5 in the UK for world-leading research in biomedical sciences, law, and creative technologies, Ulster offers comprehensive doctoral funding and strong NHS partnerships.

Verified degree programs and opportunities

Review checked academic pathways and funding options below:

Department for the Economy (DfE) Vice-Chancellor's PhD Studentship

Prestigious Northern Ireland government scholarship funding three years of full-time doctoral research, living costs, and research materials.

Master of Science in Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning

Cutting-edge master's degree exploring deep neural networks, natural language processing, computer vision, and cognitive robotics at the new Belfast campus.

Biomedical Sciences Research Institute (BMSRI) Diabetes & Oncology Doctoral Fellowship

Three-year doctoral fellowship in the UK top-ranked Biomedical Sciences Research Institute discovering peptide-based therapeutics for type 2 diabetes and neurodegenerative disorders.

Belfast XR and Virtual Production Studio Creative Technologies Project

Creative research placement in the Studio Ulster virtual production complex pioneering real-time in-camera visual effects and immersive digital performance.

Connected Health & Medical Device Engineering Practicum

Six-month clinical engineering internship working alongside Belfast medical device manufacturers developing wearable biosensors and remote cardiovascular monitoring systems.

Transitional Justice Institute (TJI) Postdoctoral Research Fellowship

Two-year postdoctoral fellowship at the internationally acclaimed Transitional Justice Institute researching peace accords, constitutional repair, and human rights accountability.

Lecturer in Criminology and Restorative Justice

Permanent academic lectureship in the School of Applied Social and Policy Sciences teaching undergraduate criminology and researching community policing models.

Admissions, language requirements & application process

Master's admission eligibility
UK 2:2 or 2:1 Honours degree (or international equivalent with minimum 55–60% depending on tier of undergraduate university).
Doctoral admission eligibility
UK Master's degree with Merit or Distinction (or First Class Honours Bachelor degree) with strong research proposal and supervisory alignment.
Language proficiency
IELTS Academic 6.0 overall with no band below 5.5 (or IELTS 6.5 for clinical/health disciplines).
Research statement / Proposal
Detailed research proposal (approx 1,000–1,500 words) defining problem, methodology, and relevance to Ulster research institutes.
Application fee
£0 (Free direct online postgraduate application portal).
Required documentation
Official academic transcripts, degree awards, English language qualification, personal statement, and two academic references.

Tuition fees and verified scholarship systems

International postgraduate taught tuition typically ranges from £16,320 to £20,500 per academic year. Fully funded doctoral research studentships (supported by the Department for the Economy - DfE, EPSRC, and Vice-Chancellor's Research Scholarships) provide 100% international tuition coverage plus an indexed tax-free annual stipend of £19,237.
