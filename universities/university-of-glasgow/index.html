University of Glasgow (Gilmorehill, Glasgow)

Founded in 1451 by papal bull, the University of Glasgow is the fourth-oldest university in the English-speaking world and a core member of the Russell Group. Ranked consistently among the top 80 universities globally, Glasgow was the epicenter of the Scottish Enlightenment, boasting illustrious historical figures such as Adam Smith (father of modern economics), James Watt (inventor of the steam engine), and Lord Kelvin (pioneer of thermodynamics). Situated on its iconic neo-Gothic Gilmorehill campus, Glasgow offers world-class funded PhD studentships, advanced Master's programs, and distinguished research leaders. Verified degree programs and opportunities

Review checked academic pathways and funding options below:

Glasgow Quantum & Imaging Doctoral Training Studentships

Prestigious doctoral studentship scheme in the School of Physics & Astronomy, supporting research in quantum imaging, single-photon detection, and gravitational wave instrumentation at the Institute for Gravitational Research.

Glasgow Master of Science (MSc) in Data Science

One-year intensive Master of Science program teaching machine learning, deep learning, big data architectures, information retrieval, and human-in-the-loop data science.

Glasgow Adam Smith Business School PhD in Economics & Finance

Four-year doctoral program at the historic Adam Smith Business School investigating macroeconomic dynamics, financial econometrics, and behavioral asset pricing.

Glasgow MRC-University of Glasgow Centre for Virus Research (CVR) Postdoctoral Associate

Two-year postdoctoral position at the UK's flagship Centre for Virus Research investigating viral evolution, host innate immune evasion mechanisms, and structure-guided antiviral designs.

Glasgow Master of Science (MSc) in Aerospace Engineering

One-year advanced degree accredited by the Royal Aeronautical Society, exploring autonomous flight controls, space propulsion systems, and aero-elastic structural design.

Glasgow School of Chemistry Undergraduate Summer Research Fellowship

Eight-week hands-on summer research placement in the Joseph Black Building investigating transition metal catalysis, chemical origin of life systems, and porous framework materials.

Glasgow Lecturer / Assistant Professor in Climate Adaptation & Environmental Justice

Permanent tenure-track academic position at the School of Geographical & Earth Sciences conducting research in climate vulnerability and teaching undergraduate and postgraduate geography cohorts.

Admissions, language requirements & application process

Master's admission eligibility
UK 2:1 Honours degree (or international equivalent, typically minimum 3.3/4.0 GPA or 65%+ UK mark equivalent) in an aligned discipline.
Doctoral admission eligibility
Master's degree with Merit or Distinction (or UK 1st Class Honours undergraduate degree) with demonstrable research expertise and supervisor agreement.
Language proficiency
Academic IELTS 6.5 to 7.0 overall (minimum 6.0 to 6.5 in all sub-tests depending on faculty band) or TOEFL iBT 90 to 100.
Research statement / Proposal
Comprehensive 1,500 to 2,000-word research proposal describing original research questions, literature context, methodological design, and prospective Glasgow supervisor alignment.
Application fee
£0 to £60 application fee for taught postgraduate courses; £0 application fee for research degrees via the Glasgow online application portal.
Required documentation
Official academic transcripts, certified English translations, degree certificates, CV, 2 academic reference letters, and research proposal.

Tuition fees and verified scholarship systems

International doctoral students are supported by University of Glasgow College Doctoral Scholarships and UKRI doctoral training partnerships. Full scholarship recipients receive a 100% full international tuition fee waiver, an annual tax-free living stipend at national UKRI rates (£21,237 per year for 3.5 to 4 years), and an annual research support grant.
