Erasmus University Rotterdam (Rotterdam, Netherlands)

Established in 1913 in Rotterdam, South Holland, Erasmus University Rotterdam (EUR) is an internationally celebrated European research university consistently ranked in the global top 70. Named after the Renaissance humanist Desiderius Erasmus, EUR is world-famous as the home of modern econometrics (Jan Tinbergen, recipient of the first Nobel Prize in Economics 1969; Tinbergen Institute), one of Europe's largest academic hospitals (Erasmus MC), and world-leading behavioral data science. Joint graduate institute of Erasmus University, University of Amsterdam, and VU Amsterdam, providing elite research training in econometrics, finance, and macroeconomics.

Paid academic teaching positions for master's and advanced bachelor's students, leading tutorial sessions, assisting computer lab classes, and mentoring junior scholars.

Admissions, language requirements & application process

Master's admission eligibility
Accredited Bachelor of Science degree from a recognized research university (WO) with equivalent to a Dutch university GPA (typically GPA 3.2+ / 4.0 or 75%+).
Doctoral admission eligibility
Completed Master of Science degree in relevant discipline; appointments occur via competitive application to advertised salaried PhD vacancies or approved external fellowships.
Language proficiency
Demonstrated English proficiency: Academic IELTS 6.5-7.0 overall (minimum 6.0 in all subscores) or TOEFL iBT 90-100 overall (minimum 22 in all subscores).
Research statement / Proposal
Detailed motivation letter and scientific exposé explaining project methodology and research alignment.
Application fee
€100 non-refundable application fee for international students with non-Dutch diplomas.
Required documentation
Complete certified academic transcripts, degree certificate, CV, 2 academic reference letters, and statement of research motivation.

Tuition fees and verified scholarship systems

Tuition is charged for master's programs (€16,000 to €22,000/year for non-EEA students, with Holland Scholarships providing fee reductions). Doctoral candidates (Promovendi) are not students but salaried university staff employed under the Collective Labour Agreement for Dutch Universities (CAO-NU), earning gross monthly salaries of €2,872 (Year 1) rising to €3,670 (Year 4), plus 8% holiday allowance and 8.3% end-of-year bonus.
