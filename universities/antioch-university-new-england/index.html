Antioch University New England: Environmental Studies, Clinical Psychology & Faculty Guide

Established in 1964 in southwestern New Hampshire, Antioch University New England (AUNE) is a nationally recognized graduate center of Antioch University, accredited by the Higher Learning Commission (HLC). Internationally celebrated as an early pioneer of environmental sustainability, social justice, and practitioner-scholar clinical training, AUNE delivers renowned Master's and APA-accredited doctoral programs in Environmental Studies, Clinical Psychology (Psy.D.), and Education.

Verified degree programs and opportunities

Review checked academic pathways and funding options below:

Horace Mann Environmental Stewardship and Social Justice Merit Grant

Institutional scholarship honoring Antioch founder Horace Mann, supporting incoming master's and doctoral scholars in environmental studies and clinical psychology.

Master of Science in Environmental Studies (Conservation Biology Concentration)

Two-year graduate degree combining rigorous ecological fieldwork, wildlife population biology, conservation genetics, and regional biodiversity policy in northern New England.

Doctor of Psychology (Psy.D.) in Clinical Psychology (APA-Accredited)

Nationally accredited four-to-five year doctoral program preparing licensed clinical psychologists through intensive psychotherapy training, psychological assessment, and community mental health integration.

Center for Climate Preparedness and Community Resilience Project

Applied environmental policy research project partnering with municipal governments to design climate adaptation plans, flood mitigation strategies, and urban heat resilience policies.

Antioch Psychological Services Center Supervised Clinical Practicum

Comprehensive on-campus and regional clinical practicum providing outpatient psychotherapeutic services, cognitive assessments, and neurodevelopmental evaluations for patients in New Hampshire.

Rachel Carson Environmental Literature and Ecological Fellowship

Interdisciplinary fellowship supporting graduate students engaged in environmental communication, nature writing, and public ecological literacy in New England.

Core Faculty in Environmental Studies and Climate Justice Policy

Full-time core faculty appointment teaching graduate courses in environmental justice, climate policy, and community resilience, while advising master's and doctoral theses in Keene, NH.

Admissions, language requirements & application process

Master's admission eligibility
Baccalaureate degree from a regionally accredited institution with minimum cumulative GPA of 3.0/4.0 in relevant environmental, biological, or social science disciplines.
Doctoral admission eligibility
Master's degree or advanced bachelor's degree in psychology or related field; admission to the APA-accredited Psy.D. program requires a competitive academic record and holistic clinical interview.
Language proficiency
Proof of English proficiency (TOEFL iBT 80 or IELTS Academic 6.5 minimum) for international applicants.
Research statement / Proposal
Application submitted via Antioch online portal including personal essay detailing commitment to social justice, environmental stewardship, or clinical practice.
Application fee
USD $50 application processing fee.
Required documentation
Official academic transcripts from all post-secondary institutions, three letters of recommendation, professional resume/CV, and scholarly writing sample.

Tuition fees and verified scholarship systems

Tuition for master's programs in Environmental Studies is approximately USD $850 to $980 per credit hour (approximately USD $17,000 to $21,000 per academic year). Full-time tuition for the APA-accredited Doctor of Psychology (Psy.D.) program is approximately USD $34,500 per academic year. Antioch provides substantial institutional merit grants, Horace Mann Diversity Fellowships, and graduate research assistantships.
