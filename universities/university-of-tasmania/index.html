University of Tasmania: Degrees, Scholarships & Faculty Guide

The University of Tasmania (UTAS), founded in 1890 as Australia's fourth oldest university, is the global epicenter for marine and Antarctic science, oceanography, forestry, and wilderness medicine. Housing the world-renowned Institute for Marine and Antarctic Studies (IMAS) and the Australian Maritime College (AMC), UTAS provides unparalleled access to the Southern Ocean and polar expeditions.

Verified degree programs and opportunities

Review checked academic pathways and funding options below:

IMAS Antarctic & Southern Ocean Physical Oceanography Doctoral Scholarship

Doctoral studentship at the Institute for Marine and Antarctic Studies (IMAS), analyzing Southern Ocean circulation, Antarctic ice shelf melting, and sea-level rise using autonomous ocean gliders and satellite altimetry.

PhD in Marine Conservation Ecology & Climate Redistribution

Doctoral research examining how warming ocean currents drive poleward species shifts in temperate reef fishes and kelp forests, in collaboration with the Centre for Marine Socioecology.

PhD in Analytical Chemistry & Microfluidic Environmental Sensors (ACROSS Group)

Doctoral position within the Australian Centre for Research on Separation Science (ACROSS), engineering portable autonomous chemical analyzers for trace nutrient detection in ocean waters.

Master of Maritime Engineering (Naval Architecture & Ocean Engineering)

Two-year Master of Engineering taught at the Australian Maritime College (AMC) in Launceston, utilizing Australia's largest hydrodynamic towing tank and cavitation tunnel to design icebreaking and offshore vessels.

Master of Information Technology and Systems (Spatial Data Science)

Two-year Master's degree providing advanced skills in geospatial modeling, remote sensing, spatial databases, and automated drone telemetry mapping.

Australian Centre for Excellence in Antarctic Science (ACEAS) Postdoctoral Fellowship

Two-year postdoctoral research appointment at ACEAS headquarters in Hobart, investigating East Antarctic ice sheet tipping points and atmospheric teleconnections.

IMAS Marine Laboratories Summer Oceanographic Research Practicum

Ten-week coastal and estuarine research placement surveying Giant Kelp restoration sites and microzooplankton grazing dynamics in the D'Entrecasteaux Channel.

Admissions, language requirements & application process

Master's admission eligibility
A recognized bachelor's degree in a relevant discipline with at least a credit average (65%+) or equivalent international grade.
Doctoral admission eligibility
Bachelor's degree with First Class Honours (H1) or a Master by Research with proven research outputs; prior agreement with a qualified UTAS primary supervisor.
Language proficiency
IELTS Academic 6.5 overall (no band below 6.0) or TOEFL iBT 79 (writing 21, reading 18).
Research statement / Proposal
Comprehensive 4-5 page research proposal outlining research significance, methodologies, field study logistics, and safety protocols.
Application fee
AUD $0 for direct online research degree applications.
Required documentation
Certified transcripts and degree certificates, research proposal, CV, two academic referee reports, and supervisor confirmation.

Tuition fees and verified scholarship systems

International higher degree by research (HDR) students at UTAS are heavily supported through Australian Government RTP Scholarships and Tasmania Graduate Research Scholarships (TGRS). Awardees receive 100% full international tuition fee waivers for up to 3.5 years and an indexed living allowance of AUD $33,500 per year tax-free. International coursework tuition averages AUD $33,000–$39,000 per year.
