Brandeis University: Academic Guide, Degrees & Scholarships

Established in 1948 by the American Jewish community and named for Supreme Court Justice Louis Dembitz Brandeis, the university educates 5,500 students on a 235-acre campus in Waltham, just nine miles west of Boston. Brandeis is an elite member of the Association of American Universities (AAU), combining the personalized liberal arts mentorship of a small college with the research power of an R1 institution. Faculty members include Nobel laureates (such as Michael Rosbash and Jeffrey C. Hall for circadian rhythm discoveries) and MacArthur fellows. Landmark research thrives within the Rosenstiel Basic Medical Sciences Research Center, the Volen National Center for Complex Systems, and the Heller School for Social Policy and Management.
