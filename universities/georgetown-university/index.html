Georgetown University: Degrees, Scholarships & Faculty Guide

Georgetown University, founded in 1789 in Washington, D.C., is the oldest Catholic and Jesuit research university in the United States. Positioned at the epicentre of global diplomacy, constitutional law, and international affairs, Georgetown is world-renowned for the Edmund A. Walsh School of Foreign Service (SFS), the McCourt School of Public Policy, Georgetown Law, and cutting-edge biosecurity research.

Verified degree programs and opportunities

Review checked academic pathways and funding options below:

Doctor of Philosophy (PhD) in Government and International Affairs (Full Presidential Fellowship)

Elite five-year doctoral program within the Department of Government exploring comparative democratization, international political economy, nuclear deterrence, and global environmental regimes.

Master of Science in Foreign Service (MSFS - Walsh School of Foreign Service)

World #1 ranked master's program in international affairs, preparing future ambassadors and multilateral leaders in international development, global politics, and geopolitical trade.

Master of Science (M.S.) in Global Health and Pandemic Preparedness

Three-semester graduate degree combining epidemiology, international health law, antimicrobial resistance, and an embedded ten-week scholarly field research module in Africa, Asia, or Latin America.

Center for Security and Emerging Technology (CSET) Artificial Intelligence Policy Research Assistantship

Applied policy research residency within CSET analyzing global AI hardware competition, semiconductor export controls, military autonomous systems, and advanced foundation model safety.

Georgetown Graduate School of Arts and Sciences Diversity & Global Leadership Scholarship

Highly selective presidential scholarship awarded to incoming graduate students from historically underrepresented backgrounds who exhibit transformative scholarship and social justice leadership.

Georgetown Climate Center Federal and Multilateral Policy Practicum

Ten-week policy practicum based in Washington, D.C., drafting clean power regulatory frameworks, electric vehicle infrastructure guidelines, and coastal resilience legal briefs.

Institute for the Study of Diplomacy (ISD) Postdoctoral Fellowship in Global Technology Governance

Two-year postdoctoral fellowship at the Institute for the Study of Diplomacy examining the weaponization of economic supply chains, cyber espionage norms, and multilateral treaty negotiation.

Admissions, language requirements & application process

Master's admission eligibility
A bachelor's degree from an accredited institution with a cumulative GPA of at least 3.5 on a 4.0 scale (or equivalent) in a relevant discipline.
Doctoral admission eligibility
Undergraduate or master's degree with superior academic achievement; demonstrated research capability, competitive GRE scores (where required), and faculty alignment.
Language proficiency
TOEFL iBT minimum 100 (sub-scores 22+ in each section) or IELTS Academic minimum 7.5 (no sub-band below 7.0).
Research statement / Proposal
A 500-1,000 word Academic Statement of Purpose outlining research questions, scholarly influences, career aims, and specific Georgetown faculty mentors.
Application fee
USD $90 non-refundable application fee.
Required documentation
Official academic transcripts, 3 confidential letters of recommendation, CV/resume, personal statement, and official English test scores.

Tuition fees and verified scholarship systems

Tuition for professional master's programs at Georgetown averages USD $56,000 to $62,000 per year. Admitted doctoral candidates across the Graduate School of Arts and Sciences receive guaranteed full merit fellowships offering 100% tuition coverage, university health insurance, and annual living stipends of USD $36,000 to $42,000 for up to five years.
