University of Liverpool: Degrees, Scholarships & Faculty Guide

The University of Liverpool, founded in 1881 as one of the original 'redbrick' universities, is a founding member of the UK's Russell Group. Celebrated for nine Nobel laureates, Liverpool leads worldwide innovation in autonomous chemistry and materials discovery through the £81 million Materials Innovation Factory (MIF), particle accelerator physics at the Cockcroft Institute, and veterinary science.

Verified degree programs and opportunities

Review checked academic pathways and funding options below:

Materials Innovation Factory (MIF) PhD in Autonomous Robotics for Materials Discovery

Doctoral studentship in Professor Andy Cooper's group at the Materials Innovation Factory, developing autonomous mobile robots that conduct chemical experiments and discover photocatalysts 24/7.

PhD in Particle Accelerator Physics & Novel Beam Acceleration (Cockcroft Institute)

Doctoral research conducted within the Cockcroft Institute of Accelerator Science and Technology, designing dielectric laser accelerators and novel plasma wakefield accelerators for CERN and medical physics.

Master of Science (MSc) in Advanced Computer Science with Year in Industry

Two-year Master of Science including an optional one-year paid industrial work placement, covering formal methods, multi-agent systems, deep learning, and distributed computing.

PhD in Infectious Diseases & Antimicrobial Resistance (Institute of Infection)

Doctoral studentship at the Institute of Infection, Veterinary and Ecological Sciences, investigating mechanisms of multidrug resistance in Gram-negative bacteria and testing novel antimicrobial peptide combinations.

Master of Science in Sustainable Energy Systems and Decarbonisation

Postgraduate degree exploring smart grid integration, offshore wind turbine design, hydrogen storage systems, and industrial decarbonization strategies in the UK's North West energy corridor.

Leverhulme Research Centre for Functional Materials Design Postdoctoral Fellowship

Two-year postdoctoral position combining artificial intelligence, robotic chemistry, and computational crystal structure prediction to synthesize porous energy-storage frameworks.

Virtual Engineering Centre (VEC) Summer Digital Twin Practicum

Ten-week technical internship at the Virtual Engineering Centre in Sci-Tech Daresbury, building interactive digital twin simulations for aerospace component manufacturing.

Admissions, language requirements & application process

Master's admission eligibility
A UK 2:1 honours degree or international equivalent in an appropriate subject from a recognized university.
Doctoral admission eligibility
A UK master's degree (or international equivalent) with Merit or Distinction, or a First-Class Honours bachelor's degree; comprehensive research proposal and confirmed academic supervisor.
Language proficiency
IELTS Academic 6.5 overall (minimum 6.0 in each sub-test) or TOEFL iBT 88 (minimum 19 in listening, 20 in reading, 20 in speaking, and 21 in writing).
Research statement / Proposal
Detailed 1,000 to 2,000 word research proposal outlining context, methodology, proposed experimental design, and key literature.
Application fee
£0 for direct postgraduate research (PhD) applications through the university's online application portal.
Required documentation
Certified academic transcripts, degree certificates, CV, research proposal, two academic reference letters, statement of purpose, and English language certificate.

Tuition fees and verified scholarship systems

Full-time UKRI-funded doctoral students at the University of Liverpool receive a 100% tuition fee waiver and an indexed tax-free living stipend of at least £19,237 per year for 3.5 to 4 years. For international students on self-funded or institutional scholarship tracks, annual tuition is £24,000–£29,000 for postgraduate research and taught MSc degrees, with University of Liverpool International Excellence Scholarships providing up to £5,000 fee discounts.
