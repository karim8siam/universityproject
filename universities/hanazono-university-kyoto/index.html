Hanazono University: Zen Buddhist Heritage, Social Welfare & History Guide

Founded in 1872 by the Myoshin-ji temple of the Rinzai Zen sect in Kyoto, Hanazono University is a venerable institution internationally celebrated for the International Research Institute for Zen Buddhism (IRIZ), social welfare science, and ancient Kyoto historical archaeology.

Admissions, language requirements & application process

Master's admission eligibility
Graduation from an accredited 4-year undergraduate institution in humanities, sociology, social welfare, or philosophy.
Doctoral admission eligibility
Master's degree in Buddhist studies, literature, or sociology, accompanied by submitted master's thesis and comprehensive doctoral prospectus.
Language proficiency
JLPT N1 or N2 for Japanese-taught humanities tracks; reading comprehension of classical Sino-Japanese (Kanbun) beneficial for Zen archival studies.
Research statement / Proposal
Doctoral research prospectus (3,000 Japanese characters) detailing historical sources, textual manuscripts, and historiographical methodology.
Application fee
¥35,000 international examination fee.
Required documentation
Bachelor's/Master's graduation certificates, official academic transcripts, research plan, JLPT certificate, and academic recommendation letter.

Tuition fees and verified scholarship systems

Annual undergraduate tuition is approximately ¥1,080,000 to ¥1,260,000. Graduate tuition is approximately ¥680,000 to ¥820,000 annually. Rinzai Sect educational scholarships, Hanazono University international tuition reductions (30-50%), and Kyoto Prefectural grants are available.
