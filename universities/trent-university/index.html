Trent University: Degrees, Scholarships & Faculty Guide

Trent University is one of Canada's top undergraduate and research-active universities, situated along the Otonabee River in Peterborough, Ontario, with an expanding campus in Oshawa. Trent is internationally celebrated for the Trent Centre for Biomaterials Research (TCMR), freshwater aquatic ecology, DNA forensic identification, and leadership in Indigenous environmental studies.

Verified degree programs and opportunities

Review checked academic pathways and funding options below:

Doctor of Philosophy (PhD) in Environmental & Life Sciences (Aquatic Biogeochemistry & Northern Ecology)

Four-year doctoral candidacy in the Environmental & Life Sciences Graduate Program, investigating watershed nutrient dynamics, carbon cycling in boreal catchments, or Arctic freshwater biodiversity.

Master of Science (M.Sc.) in Applied Modelling and Quantitative Methods (Big Data & Machine Learning)

Interdisciplinary quantitative master's degree combining high-performance computing, mathematical optimization, and machine learning models applied to bioinformatics, financial systems, or sensor networks.

Trent International Graduate Leadership Scholarships and Tuition Bursaries

Merit-based scholarships awarded to incoming international master's and doctoral students demonstrating academic leadership, community service, and high-impact research potential in Canadian higher education.

Trent Centre for Materials Research (TCMR) Biomaterials & Green Chemistry Research Fellowship

Advanced applied research placement at TCMR synthesizing vegetable oil-derived bio-based polymers, biodegradable polyurethanes, and sustainable lubricants to replace petrochemical precursors.

Trent Water Quality Centre Graduate Analytical Research Internship

Four-to-six month research training residency at Canada's premier mass spectrometry facility, operating high-resolution ICP-MS and isotope ratio mass spectrometers on environmental and industrial water samples.

James Orbinski Postdoctoral Fellowship in Global Health, Ecosystem Health and Indigenous Well-Being

Two-year postdoctoral fellowship conducting participatory research on One Health frameworks, community-partnered water safety in First Nations reserves, and climate-induced zoonotic vulnerability.

Master of Science (M.Sc.) in Forensic Science & Environmental Toxicology

Rigorous two-year graduate curriculum in advanced forensic DNA sequencing, wildlife forensic analysis, trace toxicological detection, and courtroom expert witness methodologies.

Admissions, language requirements & application process

Master's admission eligibility
A four-year bachelor's degree from a recognized university with a minimum cumulative average of 77% (B+ equivalent) over the final two years of study.
Doctoral admission eligibility
Master's degree with a minimum A- (80%) average; approved research statement and formal confirmation of thesis supervision by a Trent graduate faculty member.
Language proficiency
Proof of English proficiency: IELTS Academic 6.5 overall (minimum 6.0 in all bands) or TOEFL iBT 86 (minimum 20 in writing).
Research statement / Proposal
A 3-5 page research outline specifying the inquiry questions, theoretical foundation, experimental or field methodologies, and laboratory resource requirements.
Application fee
CAD $125 non-refundable application fee.
Required documentation
Official academic transcripts, graduation credentials, CV, 3 confidential letters of recommendation, and statement of research intent.

Tuition fees and verified scholarship systems

International graduate tuition at Trent University ranges between CAD $17,000 and $24,000 per year depending on the discipline. Qualified research-stream graduate students receive guaranteed financial support packages averaging CAD $22,000 to $26,000 per year combining Graduate Teaching Assistantships (GTAs), Trent Graduate Research Fellowships, and supervisor research stipends.
