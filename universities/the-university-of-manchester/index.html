The University of Manchester (Manchester, England)

Established in 1824 in Manchester, England, The University of Manchester is a prominent founding member of the prestigious Russell Group and ranked among the world's top 35 universities. Manchester has generated 25 Nobel laureates and historic scientific breakthroughs: John Dalton's atomic theory, Ernest Rutherford's splitting of the atom, the world's first programmable stored-program computer (The Manchester Baby), and the isolation of graphene (Andre Geim and Konstantin Novoselov, Nobel Prize in Physics 2010). Verified degree programs and opportunities

Review checked academic pathways and funding options below:

Manchester President's Doctoral Scholar (PDS) Award

The University of Manchester's premier competitive doctoral award, offering full funding and bespoke presidential leadership training to the world's most promising doctoral researchers.

Manchester PhD in Graphene and 2D Materials (National Graphene Institute)

Doctoral research inside the world's leading centre for two-dimensional materials, exploring electronic, optical, and thermal properties of graphene and heterostructures.

Manchester MSc in Advanced Computer Science

Flexible, research-intensive Master of Science degree offering pathways in artificial intelligence, computer security, software engineering, and data analytics.

Manchester MSc in Aerospace Engineering

Advanced aerospace engineering master's program utilizing supersonic wind tunnels and flight simulation facilities to train engineers for global aerospace careers.

National Graphene Institute (NGI) & Henry Royce Institute Placements

The UK's national institute for advanced materials research, pioneering discoveries in 2D heterostructures, quantum transport, and membrane filtration.

Manchester Department of Computer Science Summer Research Internships

Intensive 8 to 10-week summer research placements inside Manchester computing labs, working directly under faculty guidance on cutting-edge machine learning and systems projects.

Manchester Graduate Teaching Assistantships & Demonstratorships (GTA)

Contractual academic employment for registered doctoral researchers, assisting in tutorial leadership, coding laboratory exercises, and student assessment.

Admissions, language requirements & application process

Master's admission eligibility
UK 2:1 honours degree or international equivalent (typically GPA 3.3/4.0 or 75-80%+) in computer science, engineering, or quantitative disciplines.
Doctoral admission eligibility
UK first-class or 2:1 honours degree plus Master's with merit or distinction (or international equivalent), with demonstrable independent research competence.
Language proficiency
Demonstrated English proficiency: Academic IELTS 7.0 overall (minimum 6.5 in all components) or TOEFL iBT 100 overall (minimum 22 in each component).
Research statement / Proposal
Comprehensive 2-3 page research project proposal detailing research questions, proposed methodology, theoretical context, and faculty advisor alignment.
Application fee
£60 application fee for select competitive taught master's degrees; PhD research applications are free of charge.
Required documentation
Complete official university transcripts, 2 confidential academic reference letters, personal statement of research interests, and curriculum vitae.

Tuition fees and verified scholarship systems

Tuition fees for international postgraduate research students are fully covered under the President's Doctoral Scholar (PDS) Award. Recipients receive 100% full international tuition fee coverage plus annual tax-free living stipends indexed to the UKRI rate (£19,237 to £20,500 per year for 3.5 to 4 years).
