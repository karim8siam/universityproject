Northern Institute (CDU): Degrees, Scholarships & Research Guide

Based at Charles Darwin University (CDU) in Darwin, the Northern Institute is Australia's leading regional social and policy research institute. Specializing in regional demography, Indigenous governance, tropical workforce development, and Southeast Asian policy engagement, the Institute provides globally unique field-based graduate research.

Australian Government RTP & Northern Institute Doctoral Research Scholarship

Full-coverage Australian government doctoral scholarship supporting domestic and international candidates undertaking research into regional and Indigenous development.

Master of Public Policy and Regional Development

Applied master's program analyzing governance in remote communities, resource economies, Indigenous land rights, and northern Australian development policy.

Northern Institute Indigenous Governance and Demography Doctoral Fellowship

Three-to-four year doctoral fellowship investigating Indigenous demographic transition, rural mobility, and community self-determination frameworks across northern Australia.

Northern Territory Savanna Fire Management and Carbon Abatement Project

Applied research placement evaluating customary Indigenous fire burning regimes, greenhouse gas emission reductions, and carbon market credits in northern savannas.

Northern Territory Government Policy & Regional Strategy Practicum

Policy internship embedded within Northern Territory Department of the Chief Minister analyzing remote service delivery, regional health, and regional economic policy.

Northern Institute Postdoctoral Fellowship in Regional Demography & Workforce Analytics

Two-year postdoctoral fellowship conducting spatial population modeling, migration trend analysis, and skilled workforce forecasting across regional and remote Australia.

Senior Research Fellow in Indigenous Knowledge Systems and Environmental Policy

Senior research leadership role directing externally funded research projects on Indigenous land and sea management, co-governance, and environmental policy.

Admissions, language requirements & application process

Master's admission eligibility
Accredited Australian Bachelor degree or international equivalent with a minimum Grade Point Average of 5.0 on a 7.0 scale in a relevant social science or policy field.
Doctoral admission eligibility
Honours Class I or Master by Research degree with demonstrated academic writing ability and alignment with Northern Institute research teams.
Language proficiency
IELTS Academic 6.5 overall with minimum 6.0 in each subtest (or PTE Academic 58).
Research statement / Proposal
Detailed research outline (1,500–2,500 words) addressing northern Australian development, Indigenous knowledge systems, or demographic dynamics.
Application fee
AUD $0 (Free direct online application for postgraduate research at CDU).
Required documentation
Certified academic transcripts, degree awards, research proposal, CV, and two referee reports from senior scholars.

Tuition fees and verified scholarship systems

Higher Degree by Research (HDR) candidates admitted to the Northern Institute receive Australian Government Research Training Program (RTP) scholarships or CDU University Research Scholarships, which provide a 100% international tuition waiver and an indexed living stipend of AUD $34,500 per annum.
