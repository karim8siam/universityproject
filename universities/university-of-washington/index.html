University of Washington: Degrees, Scholarships & Faculty Guide

The University of Washington (UW), situated on the shores of Lake Washington in Seattle, is a global tier-one public research juggernaut. Consistently ranking in the world's top 10 for research expenditures and innovation, UW is internationally renowned for the Paul G. Allen School of Computer Science & Engineering, the Institute for Protein Design (IPD) led by 2024 Nobel Laureate David Baker, and UW Medicine.

Verified degree programs and opportunities

Review checked academic pathways and funding options below:

Institute for Protein Design (IPD) Doctoral Fellowship in Computational Biomolecular Design

Doctoral research position situated at the Institute for Protein Design, training under Nobel Laureate David Baker's group to de novo design synthetic enzymes, targeted anticancer therapeutics, and nanoparticle vaccines using Rosetta and deep learning.

Doctor of Philosophy (PhD) in Computer Science & Engineering (Allen School)

World-leading doctoral program at the Paul G. Allen School of Computer Science & Engineering, conducting transformative research across artificial intelligence, systems, robotics, theory, and human-computer interaction.

Master of Science in Electrical & Computer Engineering (Data Science & Robotics)

Two-year Master of Science degree preparing advanced engineers in cyber-physical systems, autonomous vehicles, embedded edge AI, and clean power grid optimization.

PhD in Oceanography & Marine Biogeochemical Cycles (College of the Environment)

Doctoral research program utilizing UW's oceanographic research vessels (R/V Thomas G. Thompson) and cabled seafloor observatories to study global ocean circulation and ocean-climate feedbacks.

Master of Science in Computational Linguistics (MSCL)

Rigorous one-year Master of Science training students in large language models, syntactic parsing, semantic analysis, and speech recognition, led by pioneering faculty in NLP ethics.

Institute for Stem Cell and Regenerative Medicine (ISCRM) Postdoctoral Fellowship

Postdoctoral fellowship at ISCRM South Lake Union campus, engineering human pluripotent stem cell-derived organoids, cardiac regeneration models, and gene therapy delivery vehicles.

UW Ubiquitous Computing & Mobile Health Summer Graduate Practicum

Twelve-week paid summer research placement within the UbiComp Lab led by MacArthur Fellow Shwetak Patel, developing smartphone-based non-invasive medical screening algorithms.

Admissions, language requirements & application process

Master's admission eligibility
Accredited four-year US bachelor's degree or international equivalent with minimum cumulative GPA 3.0/4.0 (B average) in the final two years.
Doctoral admission eligibility
Four-year bachelor's or master's degree in a relevant discipline with exceptional academic credentials (typically GPA 3.6+ on 4.0 scale); prior research experience and strong faculty alignment.
Language proficiency
Proof of English proficiency: TOEFL iBT minimum 92 (minimum 26 in speaking for TA eligibility) or IELTS Academic overall band score 7.0 (minimum 7.0 in speaking).
Research statement / Proposal
Statement of Purpose (1-2 pages) describing research interests, intellectual trajectory, prior technical projects, and specific Allen School or UW faculty labs.
Application fee
$90 non-refundable graduate application fee.
Required documentation
Complete official academic transcripts, statement of purpose, CV, 3 confidential letters of recommendation, and English language test scores.

Tuition fees and verified scholarship systems

All admitted full-time doctoral (PhD) students in STEM and health sciences at the University of Washington receive full financial support, consisting of a 100% tuition waiver (worth over $35,000/year for non-residents), individual health, dental, and vision insurance under the GAIP plan, and a guaranteed living stipend of $44,000 to $51,000 per year through Research and Teaching Assistantships (RAs/TAs). Terminal Master's tuition averages $34,000/year.
