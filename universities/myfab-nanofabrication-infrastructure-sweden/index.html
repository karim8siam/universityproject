Myfab Sweden: Cleanroom Nanotechnology, Quantum Devices & PhD Guide

Myfab is the Swedish National Research Infrastructure for Micro and Nanofabrication, established and funded by the Swedish Research Council (Vetenskapsrådet). Operating three premier state-of-the-art cleanroom laboratories across Chalmers University of Technology (Nanofabrication Laboratory, Gothenburg), KTH Royal Institute of Technology (Electrum Laboratory, Stockholm), and Lund University (Lund Nano Lab), Myfab provides 5,400 square meters of class 10-10,000 cleanrooms. It serves as Sweden's technological bedrock for quantum computing, semiconductor physics, photonics, and MEMS.

Admissions, language requirements & application process

Master's admission eligibility
Graduate students access Myfab through Master of Science degrees in Applied Physics, Nanotechnology, or Materials Chemistry at consortium universities (Chalmers, KTH, or Lund).
Doctoral admission eligibility
Four-year fully funded salaried doctoral appointments in nano-optics, quantum electronics, or semiconductor engineering; requires accredited M.Sc. with minimum 120 ECTS in technical physics or electrical engineering.
Language proficiency
IELTS Academic 6.5 overall (minimum 5.5 in all bands) or equivalent English 6 certification.
Research statement / Proposal
Cleanroom user project proposal: detailed fabrication flow (lithography, etching, thin-film deposition, metrology), safety clearance, and resource feasibility plan.
Application fee
0 SEK for doctoral employment and academic cleanroom research user proposals.
Required documentation
Curriculum vitae, degree certificates, master's thesis, publications list, safety training certification, and two academic referee letters.

Tuition fees and verified scholarship systems

Doctoral researchers are full-time university employees earning approximately 32,500 to 36,000 SEK per month (0 SEK tuition, with complete Swedish medical, parental, and pension benefits). Academic research groups benefit from heavily subsidized cleanroom access rates through Swedish Research Council infrastructure grants.
