LINXS Institute: Advanced Neutron & Synchrotron X-ray Science Guide

Established in 2017 in Lund (Skåne) as an advanced institute bridging Lund University with the adjacent MAX IV synchrotron radiation facility and the European Spallation Source (ESS), LINXS (Lund Institute of Advanced Neutron and X-ray Science) is a world-leading hub for pioneering research across structural biology, soft matter physics, materials chemistry, and quantum beamline optics.

Verified degree programs and opportunities

Review checked academic pathways and funding options below:

LINXS Advanced Beamline Science International Doctoral Fellowship

Prestigious four-year doctoral fellowship hosted at LINXS in collaboration with Lund University, offering hands-on beamline experimental access at MAX IV and ESS.

Lund University & LINXS Joint Master of Science in Synchrotron and Neutron Science

Two-year specialized master's curriculum training beamline specialists in X-ray photonics, inelastic neutron scattering, small-angle scattering (SAXS/SANS), and cryo-EM.

LINXS Structural Biology Doctoral Research Candidature in Membrane Proteins

Four-year university doctoral position conducted jointly with Lund University investigating structural conformations of lipid membrane receptors using neutron reflectometry.

LINXS Soft Matter Theme Applied Inelastic Neutron Scattering Project

Frontier research project combining quasi-elastic neutron scattering (QENS) with molecular dynamics simulations to quantify glass transition relaxation in bio-polymers.

MAX IV & European Spallation Source Beamline Operations Internship

Six-month experimental internship embedded alongside staff scientists at MAX IV and ESS optimizing sample environment stages, detectors, and high-vacuum beam pipes.

LINXS Senior Postdoctoral Research Fellowship in Quantum Materials

Two-year postdoctoral research appointment investigating spin dynamics in topological superconductors and 2D van der Waals magnetic heterostructures.

Crafoord Foundation Advanced X-ray Spectroscopy Study Grant

Memorial grant supporting promising early-career researchers conducting pioneering operando spectroscopy experiments on renewable energy catalysts in Lund.

Admissions, language requirements & application process

Master's admission eligibility
Accredited Bachelor of Science or Master of Science degree (180 or 240 ECTS) in physics, chemistry, structural biology, materials science, or physical mathematics with distinction.
Doctoral admission eligibility
Direct doctoral research positions administered in collaboration with Lund University Faculty of Science and Faculty of Engineering (LTH); requiring a relevant second-cycle degree and competitive research proposal.
Language proficiency
Demonstrated English proficiency equivalent to Swedish upper secondary school English 6 (IELTS Academic 6.5 overall with no band below 5.5, or TOEFL iBT 90 overall).
Research statement / Proposal
Required for research fellowships and doctoral candidates: 2,500-word scientific proposal detailing proposed experiments utilizing neutron scattering or synchrotron X-ray beamlines.
Application fee
0 SEK application fee for research fellowships and salaried doctoral appointments.
Required documentation
Certified university diploma and transcripts of records, comprehensive CV with publication list, statement of research interests, and two academic reference letters.

Tuition fees and verified scholarship systems

Tuition is 0 SEK for all doctoral candidates and research fellows, as doctoral positions in Sweden are salaried employee appointments (doktorandanställning) earning approximately 32,500 to 36,000 SEK per month with full social security and pension coverage. Postdoctoral fellowships provide fully funded tax-free salaries.
