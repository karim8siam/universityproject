Hiroshima Shudo University: Environmental Economics, Law & Humanities Guide

Tracing its foundational origin to 1725 as the Asano clan school, Hiroshima Shudo University (Hiroshima Shudo Daigaku) is one of western Japan's most historic private universities. Situated in the hills of Asaminami-ku in Hiroshima, Shudo is celebrated for international business economics, commercial law, peace and reconciliation studies, and environmental management.

Admissions, language requirements & application process

Master's admission eligibility
Accredited four-year university bachelor's degree (or international 16-year education equivalent) with verified credits in economics, law, or humanities.
Doctoral admission eligibility
Completion of Master's degree (Shushi) in commercial sciences, law, or humanities; submission of research thesis and passing oral defense examination.
Language proficiency
Japanese language proficiency at JLPT N1 or N2 for Japanese-taught tracks; CEFR B2 / IELTS 6.0+ for designated research fellowship tracks.
Research statement / Proposal
Detailed 2,500-character Japanese (or 1,500-word English) graduate research plan detailing research questions and alignment with Shudo seminar professors.
Application fee
JPY 35,000 university graduate entrance examination fee.
Required documentation
Bachelor's diploma, academic transcript, graduation certificate, JLPT or English proficiency test report, letter of recommendation, and research proposal.

Tuition fees and verified scholarship systems

Graduate tuition at Hiroshima Shudo University is approximately JPY 680,000 to JPY 820,000 per year, plus an initial matriculation fee of JPY 200,000 in the first year. Privately financed international students receive a 30% to 50% tuition reduction scholarship through the Shudo International Student Support Program, and can access monthly JASSO stipends.
